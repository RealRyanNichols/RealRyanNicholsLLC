## Source summary

The U.S. Government Publishing Office preserves the complete transcribed interviews of former Acting Attorney General Jeffrey A. Rosen and former Acting Deputy Attorney General Richard Peter Donoghue conducted by the House Select Committee to Investigate the January 6th Attack on the U.S. Capitol.

The transcripts address several distinct subjects:

- Justice Department review of election-related allegations after the 2020 election.
- Contacts involving President Donald J. Trump and senior DOJ leadership.
- Jeffrey Clark's proposed Georgia letter and the January 3, 2021 Oval Office leadership dispute.
- Interagency planning before January 6, including the Justice Department, FBI, Department of Defense, Metropolitan Police Department, U.S. Capitol Police, U.S. Marshals Service, and ATF.
- The Justice Department response while the Capitol breach was underway.
- Calls and communications with White House staff, congressional leadership, Vice President Mike Pence, and other federal officials.

Rosen and Donoghue described their own recollections, interpretations, and actions. Their testimony is preserved as attributed firsthand government testimony, not as independent proof of every disputed allegation or characterization discussed during the interviews.

## Material metadata conflict

The GovInfo package title for Jeffrey Rosen labels the source **“Transcribed Interview of Jeffrey Rosen, (Oct. 13, 2022).”** The official transcript cover and first page state **“Wednesday, October 13, 2021.”** The archive uses October 13, 2021 as the interview date because it appears on the native transcript itself, while preserving the conflicting GovInfo title metadata.

Richard Donoghue's GovInfo title and transcript cover both support October 1, 2021.

## Attribution and verification boundary

- Statements about election allegations, White House contacts, Jeffrey Clark, preparations for January 6, and the federal response remain attributed to Rosen or Donoghue unless separately corroborated.
- Committee questions and characterizations are not treated as findings merely because they appear in the transcript.
- References to President Trump, Jeffrey Clark, other officials, agencies, or communications do not establish criminal conduct, intent, or the truth of every underlying claim.
- The archive distinguishes the witnesses' firsthand recollections from committee analysis, later reports, and editorial analysis concerning alleged government weaponization.
- No individual January 6 defendant is connected to these transcripts without person-specific evidence.
