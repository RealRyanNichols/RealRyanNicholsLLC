# BBC asks D.D.C. court to further unseal Trump executive-privilege decisions

## Why this belongs in the January 6 archive

The BBC's pending Florida defamation case concerns its edit of President Donald J. Trump's January 6, 2021 Ellipse speech. Current reporting states that the BBC has separately asked Chief U.S. District Judge James E. Boasberg in Washington, D.C., to further unseal decisions concerning executive-privilege disputes from the federal January 6 grand-jury investigation.

The requested decisions may affect whether the BBC can obtain evidence from former Trump-administration witnesses in the Florida action. This procedural request does not establish the truth of the BBC's defenses, does not itself unseal any record, and does not adjudicate any person's conduct on January 6.

## Reported July 22, 2026 filing

Law&Crime and The New Republic independently reported that the BBC filed a reply memorandum on July 22, 2026 asking Judge Boasberg to unseal the executive-privilege decisions to the greatest extent possible.

The reports attribute the following positions to the BBC:

- The BBC expects the sealed decisions to show that earlier executive-privilege assertions were rejected during special counsel Jack Smith's January 6 investigation.
- The BBC argues that those rulings could help it respond to current privilege objections in the Florida defamation case.
- The BBC identified former Vice President Mike Pence's former counsel Greg Jacob as one source from whom potentially responsive records were reportedly withheld after a White House privilege direction.

These are the BBC's litigation positions as reported from its filing. They are not independent archive findings.

## Primary background preserved

A published August 11, 2025 opinion in *In re Donald J. Trump*, No. `1:25-mc-00008-JEB`, explains that records from the election-related grand-jury investigation included sealed disputes over attorney-client privilege and other protections. Judge Boasberg ordered some attorney-client-privilege opinions, orders, and docket sheets unsealed subject to redaction, while preserving material still protected by Federal Rule of Criminal Procedure 6(e).

That 2025 opinion provides verified background for the later BBC request. It does not verify the exact docket-entry number, attachments, or complete text of the BBC's July 2026 reply.
