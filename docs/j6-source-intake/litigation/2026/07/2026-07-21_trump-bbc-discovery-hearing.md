# Trump v. BBC — July 2026 discovery and party-status developments

## Why this belongs in the January 6 archive

President Donald J. Trump's defamation case against the BBC concerns a documentary edit of his January 6, 2021 Ellipse speech. The BBC previously acknowledged that separated portions of the speech had been combined in a way that created a mistaken impression, while disputing that the edit created legal liability.

## Verified case identity and schedule

The public docket identifies *Trump v. British Broadcasting Corporation et al.*, No. `1:25-cv-25894-ALTMAN/LETT`, in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Florida. Judge Roy K. Altman is the presiding district judge and Magistrate Judge Enjoliqué A. Lett handles referred discovery matters. The scheduling order sets jury trial for February 15, 2027, subject to later court modification.

## July 16 party-status development

Multiple reports published from court filings state that Trump voluntarily dropped the claims against BBC Studios Distribution Limited and BBC Studios Productions Limited while continuing the action against the British Broadcasting Corporation. The archive has not yet captured the native July 16 stipulation, notice, or dismissal order, so this procedural change remains attributed to the reporting sources pending the court-file capture.

## July 21 discovery development

The public docket confirms that Magistrate Judge Lett scheduled four discovery disputes for hearing on July 21, 2026. They concerned:

- a subpoena to the Donald J. Trump Revocable Trust;
- forty-seven third-party subpoenas concerning January 6, 2021;
- Trump's responses and objections to document requests and interrogatories; and
- Trump's responses and objections to requests for admission.

Multiple independent reports published after the hearing state that the magistrate judge permitted financial discovery tied to the Trump trust and allowed January 6-related third-party discovery to proceed. The reports describe the financial information as relevant because the complaint placed alleged economic and reputational harm at issue. Reports also identify potential testimony or records sought from people including Donald Trump Jr., Steve Bannon, and Stephen Miller.

NOTUS and TheWrap separately reported the financial-production ruling on July 22. TheWrap quoted the court as reasoning that Trump's brand, properties, and businesses were said to have been affected. That quoted reasoning remains attributed to the reporter until a native transcript or written order is preserved.

## What this does and does not establish

The discovery rulings concern what information the parties may seek while litigating the case. They do not decide whether the BBC is liable for defamation, whether Trump suffered legally compensable harm, whether the documentary reached a legally relevant audience in Florida, or whether any person acted unlawfully on January 6.

The reported July 16 dismissal of the BBC subsidiaries narrows the defendant list; it does not resolve the remaining claim against the British Broadcasting Corporation.
