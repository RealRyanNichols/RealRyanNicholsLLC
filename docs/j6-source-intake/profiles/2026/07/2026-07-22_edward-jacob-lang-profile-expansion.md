# Edward Jacob “Jake” Lang — profile expansion source bundle

## Scope

This bundle supports the July 22, 2026 expansion of Edward Jacob Lang’s January 6 archive profile. It separates:

- charges and government allegations;
- detention and appellate rulings;
- the Section 1512(c)(2) litigation that reached the Supreme Court;
- the January 2025 executive action and district-court dismissal; and
- Lang’s attributed post-release statement.

Lang did not reach a criminal trial or receive a January 6 conviction. The pending indictment was dismissed with prejudice on January 21, 2025 after the presidential proclamation directed the Justice Department to seek dismissal of pending January 6 indictments. Public shorthand frequently calls every covered person “pardoned,” but the controlling disposition in Lang’s criminal case is dismissal of the pending indictment, not a judgment of conviction followed by a pardon.

## Verified procedural record

1. **January 16, 2021 — Arrest.** The salvaged Justice Department defendant record lists Lang’s arrest date as January 16, 2021.
2. **January 29, 2021 — Indictment.** The DOJ record and later appellate opinion identify the initial indictment and later superseding charges.
3. **January 12, 2022 — Detention ruling affirmed.** The D.C. Circuit affirmed the district court’s September 20, 2021 denial of pretrial release in Case No. 21-3066.
4. **June 7, 2022 — Section 1512(c)(2) count dismissed.** Judge Carl J. Nichols dismissed the obstruction count under the district court’s then-narrow reading of the statute.
5. **April 7, 2023 — D.C. Circuit reversed.** In the consolidated *Fischer* appeal, the D.C. Circuit reinstated the obstruction count against Fischer, Lang, and Miller.
6. **July 2, 2024 — Supreme Court vacated and remanded Lang’s appellate judgment.** The Court granted Lang’s petition, vacated the D.C. Circuit judgment, and remanded for reconsideration in light of *Fischer v. United States*.
7. **January 20–21, 2025 — Executive action, dismissal, and release.** The presidential proclamation directed dismissal of pending January 6 indictments. The district court granted the government’s motion and dismissed Lang’s indictment with prejudice on January 21, 2025. He was released from D.C. custody that day after more than four years in pretrial detention.
8. **January 2025 — Attributed statement.** In a recorded post-release clip carried in a CNN transcript, Lang said that he and other January 6 defendants would be “vindicated in the pages of history.” This is preserved as his statement, not as an archive finding.
