# Julian Elie Khater profile expansion

## Verified procedural record

- March 14, 2021: Khater was arrested at Newark Liberty International Airport. The Justice Department announced the arrest on March 15.
- March 17, 2021: a federal grand jury returned an indictment in the District of Columbia.
- May 12, 2021: Judge Thomas F. Hogan ordered Khater detained pending trial.
- July 26, 2021: the D.C. Circuit affirmed the detention order in *United States v. Khater*, No. `21-3033`.
- September 1, 2022: Khater pleaded guilty to two felony counts under 18 U.S.C. §111(a)(1) and (b).
- January 27, 2023: Judge Hogan imposed 80 months of imprisonment, a $10,000 fine, and $2,000 restitution.
- January 20, 2025: Khater’s covered convictions fell within the proclamation’s full-pardon provision because he was not among the fourteen named commutation recipients.

## Material corrections

### Arrest date

The prior archive profile listed March 15, 2021, which was the date of the Justice Department announcement. The official arrest record states that Khater was arrested on March 14, 2021. The profile now uses March 14.

### Supervised-release conflict

The Justice Department’s January 27, 2023 sentencing press release states that Khater received 36 months of supervised release. A separate Justice Department January 6 sentencing table and the archive’s salvaged dataset report 24 months.

The native judgment is the controlling record and has not yet been captured. The profile preserves the conflict rather than silently choosing one term.

### Clemency classification

The January 20, 2025 proclamation commuted the sentences of fourteen named people and granted full, complete, and unconditional pardons to all other people convicted of covered January 6 offenses. Khater was not named in the commutation list. His profile now classifies the relief as a full pardon rather than using an imprecise generic clemency label.

## Source posture

The Justice Department’s complaint-stage descriptions remain allegations unless incorporated into a plea or adjudicated record. The D.C. Circuit judgment addressed pretrial detention and did not determine guilt. The profile separately identifies the two offenses to which Khater pleaded guilty.

The U.S. Capitol Police accepted the D.C. medical examiner’s finding that Officer Brian Sicknick died of natural causes. Khater was not charged with causing Officer Sicknick’s death. The assault record and medical cause-of-death finding must not be conflated.
