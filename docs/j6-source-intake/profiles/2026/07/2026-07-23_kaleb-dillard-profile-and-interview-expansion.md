## Verified procedural record

1. DOJ reported that Kaleb Dillard was arrested in Columbiana, Alabama, on August 23, 2022. The criminal complaint alleged two felony offenses and six misdemeanor offenses. Complaint-stage charges remain allegations.
2. DOJ reported that Dillard pleaded guilty on July 18, 2023, before U.S. District Judge Jia M. Cobb to one felony count of assaulting, resisting, or impeding certain officers under 18 U.S.C. § 111(a)(1).
3. DOJ reported that Judge Cobb sentenced Dillard on November 16, 2023, to ten months of imprisonment, twelve months of supervised release, and $36,238.55 in restitution.
4. The January 20, 2025 presidential proclamation granted full pardons to covered January 6 defendants who were not among the fourteen people specifically named for sentence commutations. Dillard was not listed among the fourteen commutation recipients.

## Firsthand-source additions

### WBRC interview — May 26, 2026

WBRC published a James Giles interview with Dillard addressing his plea, incarceration, faith, family, accountability, and the then-proposed federal compensation fund. Statements are preserved as Dillard's attributed firsthand position and are not converted into independent archive findings.

Dillard acknowledged that he committed a crime and said he would have been willing to apologize directly to the officer. He rejected describing himself as a hero, martyr, or political prisoner, while arguing that January 6 punishments were often disproportionate. He described serving roughly eight months, including approximately seven months at FCI Yazoo City followed by halfway-house placement in Birmingham. He also described his Christian faith and marriage as central to his post-case life.

The interview discussed a proposed Anti-Weaponization Fund. Later DOJ court representations stated that the proposed federal fund had not been established and was not going forward. The interview therefore must not be presented as evidence that an active compensation program or application process exists.
