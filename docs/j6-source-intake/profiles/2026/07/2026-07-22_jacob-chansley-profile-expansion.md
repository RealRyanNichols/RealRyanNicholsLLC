# Jacob Anthony Chansley profile expansion

## Verified procedural record

- January 9, 2021: the U.S. Attorney's Office announced Chansley's arrest and initial charges.
- January 11, 2021: a federal grand jury indicted him in the District of Columbia.
- September 3, 2021: Chansley pleaded guilty to one felony count of obstruction of an official proceeding under 18 U.S.C. § 1512(c)(2).
- November 17, 2021: Judge Royce C. Lamberth sentenced him to 41 months of imprisonment, three years of supervised release, and $2,000 restitution.
- January 20, 2025: the presidential proclamation granted a full, complete, and unconditional pardon to covered January 6 defendants who were not among the fourteen named commutation recipients. Chansley was not named in the commutation list and was therefore within the proclamation's pardon provision.

## Source posture

The descriptions of January 6 conduct in the Justice Department releases are government descriptions based on the plea record and court documents. They are preserved as attributed source statements and are not used as conclusions about Chansley's moral character.

A presidential pardon is an exercise of executive clemency. It does not by itself expunge a court record or operate as a judicial order vacating a conviction. The profile should not use "vacated," "dismissed," or "erased" unless a native court order establishes that result.

## Fischer context

The Supreme Court's June 28, 2024 decision in *Fischer v. United States* narrowed 18 U.S.C. § 1512(c)(2), the sole offense to which Chansley pleaded guilty. The Justice Department's Fischer fact sheet stated that the ruling most significantly affected defendants whose only felony conviction was § 1512(c)(2). Chansley fits that general category, but this intake did not locate a Chansley-specific post-*Fischer* order vacating or modifying his judgment. The profile therefore records the legal context without inferring a case-specific result.
