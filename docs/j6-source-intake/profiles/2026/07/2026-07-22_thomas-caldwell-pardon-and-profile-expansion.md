## Verified chronology

- **January 19, 2021:** Caldwell was arrested in Virginia, according to the Justice Department's verdict release.
- **November 29, 2022:** A jury found Caldwell guilty of obstruction of an official proceeding and tampering with documents or proceedings. Public court reporting states that the jury acquitted him of seditious conspiracy and the other conspiracy counts. The Justice Department's descriptions of the broader alleged Oath Keepers plan remain attributed government claims.
- **June 28, 2024 and afterward:** The Supreme Court narrowed 18 U.S.C. § 1512(c)(2) in *Fischer v. United States*. The district court later dismissed Caldwell's obstruction conviction, leaving the evidence-tampering conviction for sentencing.
- **January 10, 2025:** Judge Amit P. Mehta sentenced Caldwell to time served, reflecting approximately 53 days previously spent in custody, with no additional supervised release reported.
- **January 20, 2025:** President Donald J. Trump's January 6 clemency proclamation specifically named Thomas Caldwell among 14 people whose sentences were commuted to time served. This was a commutation, not yet the later individual full pardon.
- **March 20, 2025:** President Trump granted Thomas Edward Caldwell a separate full and unconditional pardon for the remaining offense in *United States v. Rhodes, III et al.*, No. 1:22-cr-15. The Office of the Pardon Attorney lists the offense as tampering with documents or proceedings.
