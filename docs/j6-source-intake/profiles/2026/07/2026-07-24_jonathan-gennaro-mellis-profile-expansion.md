## Verified procedural record

- February 11, 2021: FBI statement of facts filed in magistrate case `1:21-mj-00224-ZMF`.
- February 16, 2021: Mellis arrested in Williamsburg, Virginia.
- March 10, 2021: ten-count indictment filed as ECF No. 7.
- November 10, 2021: superseding indictment filed as ECF No. 25.
- December 31, 2021: defense motion for reconsideration of detention filed as ECF No. 27.
- January 7, 2022: government opposition filed as ECF No. 28.
- February 1, 2022: defense reply filed as ECF No. 29.
- June 12, 2023: guilty plea to one count under 18 U.S.C. §§ 111(a)(1) and (b), according to the official DOJ sentencing release.
- December 20, 2023: Judge Randolph D. Moss imposed 51 months imprisonment, 36 months supervised release, and a $20,000 fine, according to the official DOJ release.
- January 20, 2025: the conviction was covered by the proclamation’s full-pardon provision.
- The Office of the Pardon Attorney later listed `MELLIS, JONATHAN GENNARO` as a person who requested and was issued a pardon certificate.

- The statement of facts and both indictments contain government allegations. The indictment was not the final disposition of all ten counts.
- The defense motion and reply present defense advocacy, including arguments about Mellis’s intent, Rosanne Boyland and footage concerning Officer Michael Fanone. Those claims are attributed to the filings and are not converted into independent archive findings.
- The government opposition presents prosecutorial advocacy and is preserved alongside the defense filings.
- The DOJ sentencing release is used for the verified plea date and sentence. Its narrative description of conduct remains attributed to DOJ and referenced court documents.
- The signed judgment has not been captured. The profile therefore records the sentence terms confirmed by DOJ but does not add other monetary terms from secondary summaries.
- The pardon recipient page verifies list inclusion. It is not a substitute for the person-specific certificate binary.
- Photographs displayed by third-party publishers or identification projects were not copied because original provenance and lawful reuse rights were not established.
