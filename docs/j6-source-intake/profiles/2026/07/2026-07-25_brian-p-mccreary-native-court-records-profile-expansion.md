## Source-specific findings and boundaries

### FBI affidavit

The affidavit states that a January 7 tip under McCreary's name included video clips and written descriptions. It recounts a January 26 voluntary FBI interview and statements attributed to McCreary concerning his entry, exit after police direction, reentry, and recordings. These are probable-cause allegations and attributed statements, not final findings.

### Indictment, plea, and judgment

The indictment charged five counts. The plea agreement and statement of offense establish a guilty plea to Count 2, entering or remaining in a restricted building or grounds under 18 U.S.C. § 1752(a)(1). The signed judgment records dismissal of Counts 1, 3, 4, and 5 on the government's motion.

### Government sentencing position

The government requested three months of home detention, 36 months of probation, 60 hours of community service, and $500 restitution. It stated that McCreary pleaded at the earliest available opportunity, was candid during a voluntary interview, supplied valuable video evidence within twenty-four hours, and did not personally engage in violence or property destruction. The same memorandum argued that his conduct remained serious. These statements are the prosecution's sentencing advocacy.

### Defense sentencing position

The defense requested 18 months of probation, 150 hours of community service, and $500 restitution without home detention. It emphasized remorse, early cooperation, family and employment history, lack of prior convictions, and asserted lack of violent planning or extremist-group affiliation. These statements are defense advocacy.

### Firsthand allocution and judicial ruling

During the sentencing hearing, McCreary described his intent to record material for a possible podcast, said his understanding changed on the drive home, stated that he contacted the FBI immediately after returning home, and expressed remorse. Those statements are attributed firsthand statements in the court transcript.

Chief Judge Howell found that the conduct warranted intermittent custody despite the cooperation. The signed judgment imposed:

- 36 months of probation;
- 42 days of intermittent confinement in three 14-day periods;
- two months of home detention;
- a $2,500 fine;
- a $25 special assessment; and
- $500 restitution.
