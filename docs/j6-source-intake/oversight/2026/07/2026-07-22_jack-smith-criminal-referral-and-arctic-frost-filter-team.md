## Why this source package matters

Three official records materially expand the January 6 oversight archive:

1. A July 14, 2026 Justice Department letter states that the Special Counsel investigative team directly accessed National Archives text-message spreadsheets involving 44 current or former members of Congress before the established filter-team review process was completed.
2. A 90-page exhibit released with the July 14 disclosure contains internal filter-team presentations, rosters, memoranda, review statistics, and August 21, 2023 emails describing a single National Archives production of 54 Excel files containing White House phone text messages. The emails record investigative-team downloading, circulation, transfer, and review-system loading.
3. A July 22, 2026 letter from House Judiciary Committee Chairman Jim Jordan asks the Justice Department to investigate whether former Special Counsel Jack Smith violated 18 U.S.C. § 1001 during a December 17, 2025 deposition.

The third document is a congressional criminal referral. It is not a criminal charge, indictment, conviction, prosecution decision, or judicial finding. Jack Smith's attorneys and House Judiciary Ranking Member Jamie Raskin dispute the referral and maintain that Smith answered truthfully about toll-record subpoenas, which they distinguish from the National Archives production.

## Native Exhibit A findings

The 90-page PDF is titled `Arctic Frost Filter Review Released by Grassley and Johnson`.

The package identifies:

- `Project Coconut` as the January 6 investigation.
- `Project Cranberry` as the Mar-a-Lago documents investigation.
- A segregated filter-team environment intended to keep potentially privileged or nonresponsive material from the investigative team until review.
- Internal rules stating that filter-team members generally would not be part of or communicate with the investigative team except through a designated coordinator.

Pages 72–84 preserve August 21, 2023 email chains concerning a National Archives production. One email describes a `single production consisting of 54 excel files with text messages from White House phones`. The emails record personnel downloading the files, circulating storage locations, directing that the production be copied for litigation-support processing, loaded into the NARA folder in Everlaw, and considered for discovery.

The associated four-page DOJ summary states that investigators accessed the spreadsheets before the established filter-team review was completed and identifies communications involving 44 current or former members of Congress. The exhibit supplies underlying internal process and transfer records; it does not independently adjudicate criminality, privilege, or the legality of the underlying records request.
