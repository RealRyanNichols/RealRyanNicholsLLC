## Court-record corroboration

A docket-extracted copy of Judge Reggie B. Walton’s October 15, 2025 order in *United States v. Morrissey* confirms the following procedural history:

1. Morrissey pleaded guilty on **February 23, 2022** to parading, demonstrating, or picketing in a Capitol Building under 40 U.S.C. § 5104(e)(2)(G).
2. On **August 16, 2022**, the court imposed 45 days of incarceration, 36 months of probation, a $2,500 fine, $500 restitution, and a $10 special assessment.
3. Morrissey appealed on **August 23, 2022**.
4. The D.C. Circuit remanded for resentencing on **February 1, 2024** in light of *United States v. Little* and its rule that imprisonment and probation generally cannot be combined.
5. On **April 10, 2024**, the district court resentenced Morrissey to three years of probation.
6. Morrissey filed a 28 U.S.C. § 2255 motion in November 2024. The court denied that motion as moot on **January 30, 2025** after the presidential pardon.
7. On **October 15, 2025**, the court denied without prejudice the portions of Morrissey’s Rule 60(b)(3) motion seeking damages against former counsel and the prosecutor, held the remaining portions in abeyance, and denied without prejudice his emergency request for interim monetary relief.

The October 15 order records Morrissey’s allegations concerning Brady material, ineffective assistance, and alleged fraud as claims made in his motions. The order does not establish those allegations as proven facts.

## Pardon status and attributed rejection claim

The Office of the Pardon Attorney’s public certificate-recipient list includes **MORRISSEY, DANIEL MICHAEL**. The October 15, 2025 order also states that a presidential pardon had issued and that the court denied the pending § 2255 motion as moot after that pardon.

In the Todd Huff interview and a January 2026 self-authored article, Morrissey says he notified the Office of the Pardon Attorney on **June 9, 2025** that he rejected or returned the pardon. That is preserved as Morrissey’s firsthand legal position. The available sources reviewed in this intake do not establish a final judicial ruling on whether that attempted rejection altered the pardon’s legal effect.
