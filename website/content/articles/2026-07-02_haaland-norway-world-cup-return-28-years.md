Twenty-eight years.

That's how long Norway went without qualifying for a men's World Cup. An entire generation of Norwegian kids grew up watching other countries play in the tournament their own nation used to be a fixture of, wondering if it would ever happen for them.

Then Erling Haaland scored twice in Norway's opener, a 4-1 win over Iraq, to mark his own World Cup debut ([Al Jazeera's coverage of the match](https://www.aljazeera.com/sports/2026/6/17/haaland-scores-two-goals-as-norway-beat-iraq-4-1-on-world-cup-return)). He turned in the opener from close range, watched Iraq answer back, then pounced on a defensive mistake to put Norway back in front before halftime. Then he did it again in the next match, scoring another brace against Senegal.

Two matches. Two braces. A 28-year wait, answered in about 180 minutes of football.

X did what X does with a moment like that — clips of Haaland's celebrations, of Norwegian fans back home losing their minds in packed squares, of pundits recalculating the Golden Boot race with Haaland suddenly one goal behind Messi, all inside 48 hours of kickoff.

## The wait is the whole story

Here's what gets lost if you only watch the highlight reel: Haaland didn't inherit a program that expected this. He inherited a country that had stopped assuming it belonged on that stage.

Twenty-eight years is long enough that most of the players on this Norway roster have no personal memory of their country in a World Cup. They grew up as outsiders to the tournament their nation once played in. That does something to how a team walks onto the pitch. Either it crushes you, or it turns into fuel.

Norway looked like a team that turned it into fuel.

## Why this hits different than a "normal" World Cup run

Most World Cup stories are about favorites confirming they're favorites, or dark horses stealing one big result before the run ends. This is neither. This is a country that had to sit out an entire generation of the sport's biggest stage, get back in, and then have its best player show up looking like he'd been saving up five years of frustration for exactly this moment.

I don't think it's a coincidence that Haaland — one of the most dominant strikers of his generation — spent his prime years unable to play in the one tournament that defines a career the most. He built his legend everywhere else: the Premier League, the Champions League, every individual honor available to him. The one thing missing was this stage, because his country simply wasn't on it.

Now it is. And he's scoring like a man who knows exactly how much time was lost.

## What I keep coming back to

There's a version of this story where Norway missing 28 years of World Cups becomes an excuse — a reason to play tight, to play scared, to treat the return as fragile, like something that could slip away if they're not careful.

Instead they're playing like a team that decided the only thing to do with lost time is make the time they finally got count for double. Two braces in two matches isn't cautious football. It's a team, and a player, refusing to treat a hard-won second chance like anything less than exactly that.

That's a mindset worth more than the goals themselves.
