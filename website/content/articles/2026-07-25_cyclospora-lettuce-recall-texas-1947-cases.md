Federal health officials have expanded a Cyclospora outbreak investigation to nine states and at least 1,947 illnesses. The recall matters in Texas because recalled iceberg-lettuce products were distributed here—even though Texas is **not** currently listed among the nine states with confirmed cases in this particular outbreak.

That distinction is the heart of the story: distribution is not the same thing as a confirmed case, and a recalled product is not proof that every package was contaminated. But the products, dates and symptoms are specific enough that Texans can make a useful check tonight.

The most recent [FDA outbreak update](https://www.fda.gov/food/outbreaks-foodborne-illness/investigation-9-state-outbreak-cyclospora-illnesses-iceberg-lettuce-july-2026), dated July 24, reports 1,947 illnesses, at least 98 hospitalizations and no deaths. Illness onset dates range from June 22 through July 20. The investigation remains open, and FDA says case totals may continue to rise because confirming whether a person belongs to the outbreak can take as long as six weeks.

The [CDC advisory](https://www.cdc.gov/cyclosporiasis/outbreaks/07-26/index.html) says the outbreak is linked to iceberg lettuce and that Taylor Farms de Mexico recalled all iceberg lettuce sourced from central Mexico on July 17. FDA says its traceback work and epidemiological data converge on shredded iceberg lettuce from that supplier.

Federal officials also say 1,947 infected people in the nine states reported exposure to Taco Bell. Taco Bell told FDA it had stopped using lettuce from Taylor Farms de Mexico as of July 17. That does not mean every Taco Bell location or meal was affected, and the federal advisory does not list Texas among the states with cases tied to the outbreak.

## Which recalled products should Texans check?

The [full FDA recall notice](https://www.fda.gov/safety/recalls-market-withdrawals-safety-alerts/taylor-fresh-foods-recalls-iceberg-lettuce-central-mexico-because-possible-health-risk) says shredded iceberg products were distributed from June 29 through July 16 to food-service customers in Texas and 26 other states.

The retail portion includes Marketside-brand products sold at select Walmart stores in Texas:

- Iceberg Salad in 12-ounce and 24-ounce packages, with “Best if Used By” dates from July 18 through August 3, 2026.

- Shredded Lettuce in 8-ounce and 16-ounce packages, with “Best if Used By” dates from July 18 through August 3, 2026.

The recall also covers multiple food-service brands, package sizes and date ranges. Do not try to identify those products from a social-media graphic or a remembered package color. Compare the label directly with the official FDA recall table.

If a product matches the recall, FDA and CDC say to discard it or return it for a refund. Then wash items and surfaces that touched it with hot, soapy water or in a dishwasher.

## A ten-minute recall check

1. **Check the refrigerator.** Look for packaged iceberg salad or shredded lettuce, especially Marketside products in the listed sizes and date window.

2. **Check the exact FDA table.** Match the brand, package size and “Best if Used By” date. Do not rely on a screenshot that may be incomplete.

3. **Discard or return matches.** Do not taste the lettuce to decide whether it is safe.

4. **Clean contact surfaces.** Wash the produce drawer, containers, cutting boards and utensils that may have touched a recalled product.

5. **Write down possible exposure.** If someone is ill, note when the lettuce was eaten, the store or restaurant and when symptoms began. Public-health investigators may ask what the person ate during the prior two weeks.

The [Texas Department of State Health Services recall page](https://www.dshs.texas.gov/food-manufacturers-wholesalers-warehouses/food-alerts-recalls-affecting-texas) links Texans to the federal recall and outbreak investigation.

## Symptoms and when to act

Cyclospora is a microscopic parasite that can cause an intestinal illness called cyclosporiasis. CDC lists watery diarrhea, fatigue, loss of appetite, weight loss, cramping, bloating, increased gas and nausea among common symptoms.

Symptoms usually begin about one week after infection, but CDC says the range can be from two days to two weeks or longer. Without treatment, symptoms can last from a few days to a month or longer and may appear to improve before returning.

CDC advises people with symptoms to contact a health-care provider. It also notes that routine stool tests do not always screen for Cyclospora, so a person may need to request specific testing. People with weakened immune systems, older adults and children can face greater risk from severe illness or dehydration, according to FDA.

This article is public-safety reporting, not an individual diagnosis. Severe or persistent symptoms deserve individualized medical attention.

## What remains unresolved

The investigation is moving. FDA says additional brands, restaurants, retailers or distribution channels may still be identified.

There is also an important laboratory detail. FDA initially reported a positive lettuce sample collected at the border, then reclassified that result as a false positive after expert review. FDA says that correction does not change the epidemiological and traceback basis for the recall. As of July 19, the agency reported no confirmed positive product sample.

That means the current public-health conclusion rests on converging outbreak interviews and supply-chain traceback—not a confirmed positive lettuce sample. Both facts belong in the record.

It is also possible for state totals to differ from the federal count. FDA explains that states may include probable cases or cases not yet reported to CDC, while the federal outbreak count includes confirmed cases under its current investigation definition.

## Ryan’s Take

Public accountability is not only about finding someone to blame. Sometimes it is about making the official record usable before a warning gets buried under brand names, state lists and changing numbers.

The useful Texas takeaway is narrow: recalled product reached this state, but Texas is not currently on the outbreak-case list. Check the package rather than assuming all lettuce is dangerous—or assuming Texas is unaffected.
