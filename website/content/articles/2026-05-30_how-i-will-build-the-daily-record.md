I want RealRyanNichols.com posting every day.

Not once in a while.

Not only when social media lets something breathe.

Every day.

The target is three to five posts a day when the record supports it. That only works if the system stays disciplined.

More posts cannot mean sloppier posts.

It has to mean a cleaner public record.

## The title has to tell people why it matters

A title should not be cute when the record is serious.

It should tell the reader what happened, who it involves, where it happened, or what question the post is answering.

Bad title:

> "You won't believe this."

Better title:

> "Grandparents asked for a welfare check. Now they want the records."

The second one gives the reader a person, an action, and a record question.

That is the standard.

## The first paragraph has to earn the click

The first paragraph should not wander.

It should answer one of these:

- What happened?
- Why am I posting this today?
- What record changed?
- What did someone claim?
- What proof is now available?
- What is still missing?

People should know within a few lines whether they are reading a personal update, a legal record, a local investigation, a January 6 case entry, a video note, or a correction.

## Short paragraphs are not decoration

Short paragraphs are how people read on phones.

They are also how complicated facts stay readable.

One idea at a time.

One fact at a time.

One question at a time.

That does not dumb the work down. It keeps the reader from getting lost.

## Bold text should point to the record

I use bold when it helps the reader see the structure.

Good uses:

- **What we know**
- **What we do not know yet**
- **The document**
- **The timeline**
- **The correction**
- **The next record request**

Bad use:

- bolding every angry sentence until nothing matters anymore.

If everything is emphasized, nothing is emphasized.

## Links should move people deeper

Every serious post should ask: where should the reader go next?

If the post is about January 6, link into the [J6 case work](/case).

If the post is about a video, link to the [videos page](/videos).

If the post needs more evidence, link to the [tip line](/submit).

If the work needs support, link to the [support page](/support).

The point is not just to get a page view. The point is to keep the reader moving through the record.

## Claims need labels

There are different kinds of statements.

They cannot all be treated the same.

**Confirmed fact:** supported by a record, direct evidence, or firsthand documentation.

**Allegation:** claimed by a person, party, source, screenshot, witness, or filing, but not independently confirmed yet.

**Question:** something the record raises but does not answer yet.

**Opinion:** my read on why it matters.

**Correction:** something that needed to be fixed after better information came in.

That labeling is not weakness. It is protection.

It keeps the site stronger than the people who just throw claims online and hope nobody checks them.

## The best daily post types

For this site, the best daily rhythm is not random.

It should rotate through post types:

1. **Receipt post.** One document, screenshot, video, transcript, or record, explained clearly.
2. **Timeline post.** A sequence of events in order.
3. **Question post.** A precise unanswered question that needs a record.
4. **Video post.** A direct explanation people can watch here first.
5. **Case link post.** A post that sends readers deeper into a case page, map, person, document, or timeline.

That gives the feed movement without turning it into noise.

## Corrections have to be visible

If I get something wrong, I need to fix it clearly.

Not hide it.

Not pretend it did not happen.

Fix it.

Say what changed.

Say why it changed.

Say what record corrected it.

That is how trust gets built.

## The attention has to serve the record

I want attention on this site.

I want people reading here, watching here, commenting here, sending tips here, and supporting the work here.

But attention by itself is not the mission.

The mission is to make the record clear.

If a post gets attention and makes the record clearer, that is a win.

If a post gets attention but makes the record dirtier, that is not a win.

So this is the rule:

> More posts. Better structure. Cleaner records. No invented facts.

That is how the daily feed gets built.
