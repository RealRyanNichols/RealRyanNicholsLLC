*By the Real Ryan Nichols Editorial Team*

LONGVIEW, Texas — A major private industrial project in North Longview has entered the construction period listed in a newly registered state record.

The [Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation project filing](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/TABS/Projects/TABS2026025489) for the Ninth Avenue Foods Texas plant was registered July 16, 2026. It lists July 20 as the anticipated construction start, an estimated cost of $76 million, and November 1, 2027, as the projected completion date.

That is a meaningful step beyond last year’s announcement. It is not, by itself, proof that construction began on schedule or that every permit, contractor, job, and incentive milestone is complete.

Here is what the public record establishes, who will be affected, and what East Texans should watch next.

## What the new state filing establishes

The TDLR Architectural Barriers System identifies the project as **Ninth Avenue Foods – Texas Plant**, project number **TABS2026025489**.

The official filing lists:

- **Address:** 5921 Lou Galosy Way, Longview
- **County:** Gregg County
- **Type of work:** New construction
- **Estimated cost:** $76 million
- **Scheduled start:** July 20, 2026
- **Scheduled completion:** November 1, 2027
- **Total listed area:** 288,786 square feet
- **Main facility:** A 282,386-square-foot dairy processing plant
- **Remote facility:** A 6,400-square-foot wastewater treatment facility
- **Funding:** Private funds, on private land, for private use
- **Current TDLR status:** Project registered

The location places the plant in Longview’s North Business Park area near Lou Galosy Way and Bill Stoudt Parkway.

A current report published July 17 by the [Houston Chronicle](https://www.chron.com/news/article/longview-texas-dairy-plant-22349320.php) also described construction as scheduled to begin July 20, based on the state filing.

The careful wording matters. TDLR’s record is an accessibility-project registration containing the owner’s planned dates and scope. It does not function as a live construction camera, a certificate of occupancy, or independent confirmation that crews broke ground on the listed date.

## Where the 150-job and $200 million figures come from

The larger economic promise predates the new TDLR filing.

In October 2025, the [Longview Economic Development Corporation announced](https://longviewusa.com/about/news-updates/ledco-board-approves-incentive-offer-for-ninth-avenue-foods) that its board approved a performance agreement with Ninth Avenue Foods. LEDCO said the company planned to build its third manufacturing facility in Longview, create **150 new full-time jobs**, and invest **more than $200 million over five years**.

LEDCO later described the recruitment as the [largest project in the organization’s history](https://longviewusa.com/about/news-updates/longview-economic-development-receives-2025-economic-excellence-recognition).

Those figures should not be confused with the $76 million in the new state registration.

The most reasonable reading is that $76 million represents the estimated cost of the construction scope registered with TDLR, while the more-than-$200-million figure represents the broader investment planned over five years. The sources reviewed do not provide a complete public line-by-line reconciliation of those two numbers.

The job count is also a projection tied to the performance agreement. It is not a statement that 150 people have already been hired.

## Who is affected

### Job seekers

The project could become one of Longview’s more important new manufacturing employers if the company meets the 150-job commitment.

That opportunity is not the same as a current hiring announcement. In October 2025, [KLTV reported](https://www.kltv.com/2025/10/27/water-labor-force-factored-into-dairy-facility-decision-build-longview/) that applications were not yet being accepted because construction had not started.

The sources reviewed for this update did not identify an official Longview hiring portal, opening date, wage schedule, benefits package, or complete list of job categories. Job seekers should verify openings through Ninth Avenue Foods, LEDCO, or a recognized workforce office before providing personal information to a third-party recruiter.

### Contractors and local businesses

A nearly 289,000-square-foot processing and wastewater project can involve construction trades, equipment vendors, logistics providers, maintenance contractors, food-safety services, transportation companies, and other suppliers.

The public filing does not say which contracts remain open or whether local-preference requirements apply. Businesses should treat vendor opportunities as possible—not guaranteed—and contact the company or LEDCO through official channels rather than entering the private construction site.

### Residents and drivers near North Longview

Construction of this scale can affect truck traffic, road use, noise, and activity around the North Business Park. The sources reviewed did not identify a public construction-traffic plan or announce a current road closure tied to the plant.

Residents should distinguish normal industrial construction from an official traffic advisory. Any closures or detours should be confirmed through the City of Longview or the agency controlling the affected road.

### Water and wastewater customers

Water and wastewater capacity played a central role in Longview’s recruitment.

In the October 2025 KLTV interview, LEDCO President and CEO Wayne Mansfield said Longview could provide 52 million gallons of water per day and described the plant as a significant water user. He also pointed to Longview’s ability to treat wastewater as a factor in the location decision.

The new TDLR scope confirms that the project includes a separate 6,400-square-foot wastewater treatment facility.

But the public sources reviewed do **not** specify:

- The plant’s expected average or peak daily water demand
- How much wastewater will be pretreated on site
- The characteristics of the discharge entering the municipal system
- Any planned utility upgrades and who pays for them
- Whether customer rates will be affected

Those are reasonable infrastructure questions. They should be answered with engineering documents, permits, agreements, and operating data—not guesses.

## What the record does not prove yet

As of July 21, several important questions remain open.

### Did physical construction begin July 20?

The state registration lists that date as the planned start. A dated schedule is not the same as verified on-site progress. Confirmation could come from the company, LEDCO, local permits and inspections, or documented observation from a lawful public location.

### When will hiring begin?

No official hiring launch was located in the sources reviewed for this article. Readers should be cautious of social-media posts or recruiters claiming to accept applications before an official channel is available.

### What will the jobs pay?

The 150-job commitment is public, but the sources reviewed do not provide wages, benefits, shifts, educational requirements, or the breakdown between production, technical, management, maintenance, laboratory, and logistics positions.

### What must the company deliver under the incentive agreement?

LEDCO announced a performance agreement, which indicates that the public support is tied to performance. The public announcement does not reproduce every milestone, repayment provision, reporting deadline, or enforcement term.

Residents can reasonably ask for the agreement and future compliance reports to be made easy to find.

### Will the scheduled completion date hold?

November 1, 2027, is the date in the TDLR registration. Large projects can change because of design revisions, equipment lead times, financing, weather, permitting, utility work, or contractor schedules. Any changed date should be sourced to an updated filing or official announcement.

## What readers can do next

### If you want a job

1. Monitor the [Ninth Avenue Foods website](https://www.ninthavenuefoods.com/) and [LEDCO news page](https://longviewusa.com/about/news-updates).
2. Use an official company or workforce link before submitting a Social Security number, banking information, or payment.
3. Prepare a current résumé emphasizing manufacturing, maintenance, food safety, quality control, logistics, laboratory work, automation, or supervisory experience.
4. Watch for training announcements from local workforce and education partners.

### If you live near the project

1. Follow City of Longview notices for verified road or utility impacts.
2. Record dates, locations, and photographs only from places where you are legally allowed to stand.
3. Report safety, drainage, traffic, or noise concerns to the responsible public agency with specific details.
4. Do not enter the site or interfere with workers to document progress.

### If you want accountability

1. Read the [TDLR project record](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/TABS/Projects/TABS2026025489), not only social-media summaries.
2. Request the performance agreement, amendments, and milestone reports from the public entity that maintains them.
3. Track four separate results: capital invested, full-time jobs created, water and wastewater performance, and public incentives earned.
4. Compare future claims against dated documents instead of treating the original announcement as the final result.

## Ryan’s Take

*This is the clearly labeled opinion section. It reflects the standing editorial perspective approved for this local-news lane; it is not additional reporting or a claim of firsthand involvement.*

Longview landing a project of this size is worth being excited about.

Manufacturing jobs matter. A large private investment matters. Expanding the tax base matters. Building something here instead of watching every major opportunity pass East Texas by matters.

But a press release is the beginning of the scorecard, not the end.

The community should be able to celebrate the opportunity and still ask basic questions: Did construction begin? How many local people were hired? What do the jobs pay? How much water will the facility use? What public support was earned? Did the company meet its deadlines? If a commitment changes, who explains why?

That is not hostility toward business. Clear expectations and public records protect serious companies from rumor while protecting residents from empty promises.

Welcome the investment. Help people prepare for the jobs. Give the project room to build.

Then measure what was promised against what was delivered.

Facts first. Results next.
