Here's what Meta shipped this month: a feature called Muse Image, built into Instagram, that let any user generate or remix an AI image of anyone else just by tagging their public account. If your Instagram profile was public and you were an adult, you were in it automatically. Nobody asked you. Nobody notified you. There was no toggle to find because the toggle had already been switched on, for you, by default.

Then people found out what had been done with their own faces.

{{share}}

## The backlash arrived faster than the feature did

X lit up almost immediately. One post that captured the mood, [from an account watching the fallout in real time](https://x.com/pash22/status/2070211712456773782), put it in five words: "The AI backlash is only getting started." It read less like a prediction and more like a description of something already happening in real time.

SAG-AFTRA, the union representing actors and performers, didn't hedge: "Anything other than a clear and conspicuous opt-in for these types of uses of Instagram users' images is unacceptable, and an utter miscalculation of public sentiment regarding the obvious dangers and harms inherent in such use." The talent agency CAA joined the condemnation. Within days — not weeks, not after a congressional hearing, days — Meta pulled the feature from Instagram and admitted it had "missed the mark."

## Notice what didn't happen

Nobody at Meta asked the people whose faces were now training data and remix material whether this was something they wanted. The feature shipped default-on. The correction only came after the people affected were loud enough, organized enough, and public enough to make the cost of keeping it higher than the cost of pulling it.

That's not a policy failure. That's the business model working as designed: ship the thing, see who screams, walk it back only for the platform where the screaming was loudest. Muse Image is still live inside the Meta AI app and WhatsApp. The retreat was surgical, not principled.

## Why this isn't really about AI

Strip away the "artificial intelligence" framing and this is an old story: an entity with more power than you decides what happens to your image, your name, or your likeness, and puts the burden on you to notice, object, and fight your way back to a baseline that should have been the default in the first place. Consent after the fact isn't consent. An opt-out you have to discover isn't a choice.

I run a site called "own your feed" for a reason. The people who get to decide what's true about you, what happens to your image, and what story gets told with your face shouldn't be a company betting that you won't notice in time to stop it. Every person whose photo got remixed without being asked has a version of that same feeling: something happened to your identity, and you found out after it was already done.

The backlash worked this time. It doesn't always. And "it got pulled after enough people noticed" is a much lower bar than "it should have asked first."
