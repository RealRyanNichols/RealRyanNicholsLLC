For a few weeks this summer, a German tourist named Freddy was one of the most likable people on the internet.

He was driving across small-town America for the World Cup, posting videos of diners, gas stations, and strangers who treated him like family. Millions of people who will never meet him rooted for his road trip like it was their own.

https://x.com/greg16676935420/status/2072099608139100661

Then Germany lost. And somewhere in that window, people who had spent weeks enjoying his content pivoted to combing through it — all 22,000 old posts — looking for something to be angry about.

They found a few. Stripped of the years and the context they were written in, a handful of old posts got repackaged as proof that the likable guy from the road-trip videos was secretly someone worth destroying. Add in a manufactured outrage that this apparently wasn't his *first* trip to the U.S. — something he says he'd openly mentioned from day one — and the pile-on had its narrative. Freddy deactivated his X account and moved to Instagram, saying he needed "something less toxic."

## I know this script by heart

I am not going to pretend Freddy's situation and mine are the same size. They're not. But the *mechanism* — that part I know cold.

Somebody decides, for whatever reason, that today is the day you stop being a person and start being a target. So they don't evaluate the whole of who you are. They go searching for the worst five seconds, the worst sentence, the worst photo they can find in years of your life — and they present it with zero surrounding context, because context is the enemy of a good pile-on. Then they wait for enough strangers to pile on top of it that leaving the platform starts to look like the only sane option left.

It doesn't matter if you're a tourist filming diners for fun or a defendant fighting for your life in federal court. The tool is identical. Dig until you find something. Remove the years around it. Post it as if that one frame is the whole person.

## Notice what it actually proves

Here's the part that should bother everyone, regardless of what you think of Freddy, of me, or of anyone else this has happened to: **the process worked exactly as designed, and it still produced injustice.**

Nobody had to lie. They just had to select. Take 22,000 posts, discard the ones that don't fit the story you've decided to tell, and amplify the ones that do. You don't need fabrication to manufacture a villain. You just need an audience willing to skip the "in context" step.

That should scare you more than a hoax would, because there's no fact-check that fixes it. The posts are real. The stripping-away is the lie.

## What actually breaks the cycle

Freddy didn't get canceled because he did something unforgivable. He got canceled because enough people decided, on a bad night after a bad loss, that tearing someone down was more satisfying than watching the tournament. That urge doesn't go away when the World Cup ends. It just finds its next target.

The only real defense is boring and unglamorous: ask for the context before you share the clip. Ask what year it's from. Ask what happened right before and right after. If you won't do that for a stranger's road-trip account, don't be surprised when nobody does it for you.
