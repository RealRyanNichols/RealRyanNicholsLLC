I am documenting this because it keeps happening.

Not once. Not one bad reply. Not one random stranger on a bad night. Every day.

I post about what happened to me in the D.C. Jail. I post about being suicidal in solitary confinement. I post about a lieutenant allegedly looking at me and saying, "Hope you don't die."

Then people show up under that post and prove the point.

I am not claiming every reply below is a prosecutable criminal threat. I am not pretending a screenshot is a full investigation by itself. I am saying this is the public language being aimed at me, in the open, while platforms still find reasons to lock or limit my account for far less.

## The receipt

On May 30, 2026, I preserved three public-reply screenshots from X.

They were all connected to the same broader pattern: my name, January 6, treason language, death-penalty framing, and replies that treat my past suicidal state like something people are allowed to mock or wish had ended differently.

## Screenshot 1: "the world would be better if you had"

The first screenshot shows my post about the D.C. Jail:

> "A D.C. Jail lieutenant looked at me while I was suicidal in solitary confinement and said: 'Hope you don't die.'"

Under that post, an account using the handle @tramppolitics replied in substance that it was a shame I would "lie" about hoping that, because "we all know" the world would be better if I had.

Read that carefully.

That was not a disagreement over policy. That was a reply under a suicide-and-solitary confinement post saying the better world is the one where I did not make it.

## Screenshot 2: "traitors have no rights"

The second screenshot shows a verified account under the name Kathleen Keirsbilck, handle @KKeirsbilc9584, replying under that same jail-suicide thread.

The reply says most countries hang traitors, that in America courts are backed up, and that I will get my day in court and "remember traitors have no rights."

That sentence matters.

The pattern is always the same:

1. Call a January 6 defendant a traitor.
2. Treat the word "traitor" as permission to talk about hanging, death, prison, or loss of rights.
3. Pretend it is political commentary instead of dehumanization.

I was pardoned by President Donald J. Trump on January 20, 2025. The federal charges against me were dismissed with prejudice. That does not stop them from using the same label over and over so they can justify the same punishment fantasy over and over.

## Screenshot 3: "I wouldn't call that a death threat"

The third screenshot is from a verified account using the name Chief Caucasoid, handle @COVIDcarditis.

That account replied to my post about a verified Facebook account that wrote I "should've been put to death for treason."

The reply tried to draw the line this way: it was not a death threat; it was a wish that I had been charged and convicted of treason, which may carry the death penalty.

That is exactly why I am documenting this.

They want to launder the same message through technical language. They want to say, "I am not threatening you. I am just wishing the legal system had taken you down a road that ends in death."

I am not required to pretend that distinction makes the pattern harmless.

## Screenshot 4: X locked my account for my reply

Then I tested the standard.

After the replies above, X showed me an enforcement screen saying my account, @RealRyanNichols, had been reported and automatically locked for violating its rules against violent speech.

The post X identified was my reply to the same thread:

> "@0xagonally @KKeirsbilc9584 Your side? What does that have to do with ME being abused while in custody? I didn't do that to anyone, but comments like yours are why I WILL do that and not show any mercy 'next time'."

I understand why those words can be read aggressively. I should have made the sentence cleaner.

That is not the issue.

The issue is the contrast.

The screenshots show X locking or limiting my account over that reply while the surrounding context includes people saying the world would be better if I had died, saying traitors have no rights, and trying to sanitize death-penalty rhetoric as something other than a threat.

X's own enforcement screen said my account was temporarily limited, that I could browse in read-only mode, and that I had to delete one post before the countdown continued.

That is a receipt too.
