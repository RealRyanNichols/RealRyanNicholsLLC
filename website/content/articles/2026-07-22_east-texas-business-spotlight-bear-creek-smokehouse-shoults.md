*Last verified: July 22, 2026*

MARSHALL, Texas — A business does not remain part of an East Texas community for more than 80 years by standing still.

Bear Creek Smokehouse began in 1943 when Hick and Nellie Shoults raised turkeys on their family farm to make a living. The company’s [official history](https://bearcreeksmokehouse.com/pages/about-us) says the operation later grew from that small beginning into a smokehouse known beyond East Texas.

Today, Bear Creek is still based along State Highway 154 northwest of Marshall. The business combines smoked-food production and shipping with a country general store, kitchen, weekend pit service, catering, an event venue, and public community events.

Local reporting identifies **Robbie and Tracy Shoults** as co-owners. The company’s own published material identifies Robbie as a third-generation owner, while the business describes a broader family legacy that now reaches younger generations.

This spotlight is about how one family operation kept adapting without abandoning the product, place, and story that made it recognizable.

## Who owns Bear Creek Smokehouse?

The most direct published ownership record reviewed comes from [KLTV’s October 2023 report on Bear Creek’s 80th anniversary](https://www.kltv.com/2023/10/27/webxtra-bear-creek-smokehouse-celebrates-80th-anniversary/). That report identifies Robbie and Tracy Shoults as co-owners.

Bear Creek’s official media material also describes [Robbie Shoults as the third-generation owner](https://bearcreeksmokehouse.com/blogs/media-highlights-3/the-pantry-ingredient-you-need-to-break-out-for-tender-steaks). The company spelling is **Shoults**. One recent television story rendered the surname as “Schultz,” but the business’s own materials and earlier local reporting consistently use Shoults.

This article does not attempt to assign private ownership percentages, corporate titles to every family member, or current financial values. Those details were not established by the public sources reviewed.

What is established is that the Shoults family continues to operate the business connected to the smokehouse Hick and Nellie began in 1943.

## From backyard turkeys to an East Texas destination

The official company history says Hick and Nellie started by raising and smoking turkeys as a source of income during World War II.

KLTV’s anniversary report adds useful detail: the family first sold turkeys live and freshly dressed, then built a small smokehouse later in the 1940s. The report says the operation endured recessions, a 2011 fire, the pandemic, and supply-chain problems.

Those are not minor tests for a food business.

Food production requires dependable equipment, safe handling, packaging, transportation, inventory control, and customer trust. A family company also has to manage leadership changes without losing the knowledge that customers associate with the name.

Bear Creek’s growth can be seen in the services it offers today:

- smoked turkey, brisket, pork, bacon, tenderloin, and other prepared foods;
- online ordering and shipping;
- a general store carrying Bear Creek foods and retail gifts;
- weekday and Saturday kitchen service;
- Friday and Saturday pit service, while supplies last;
- catering;
- bulk and corporate ordering;
- an event room inside the General Store and Event Center; and
- public events that bring visitors to rural Harrison County.

The [official General Store page](https://bearcreeksmokehouse.com/pages/general-store) describes the store as a **16,600-square-foot** outlet for smoked meats, gifts, food, and a countryside visitor experience. That square-foot figure comes from the business itself; this article did not independently measure the building.

## Why the business is constructive for East Texas

A positive profile should be more than a list of products. The useful question is what the business adds to the place around it.

### It turns a rural location into a destination

Bear Creek is not located in a downtown retail block or beside an interstate interchange. Its address is **10857 State Highway 154, Marshall, Texas 75670**, between Marshall and Harleton in Harrison County.

The store, kitchen, livestock, porch, event space, and food operation give people a reason to make the drive. That visitor traffic can also introduce customers to the wider Marshall and Harrison County area.

### It keeps a specialized family trade alive

The business’s story is not merely that several relatives share a last name. It is that smoking, preparing, packaging, selling, and shipping food became a transferable operating system.

Recipes alone do not sustain a company. The next generation also has to learn purchasing, production, maintenance, food safety, customer service, cash flow, staffing, marketing, and logistics.

Bear Creek’s continued operation shows the value of transferring both craft and management.

### It creates spaces for other people to gather

The [current venue page](https://bearcreeksmokehouse.com/pages/venue) says the General Store and Event Center accommodates lunch groups, hourly rentals, and private evening events. The company also publishes [catering information](https://bearcreeksmokehouse.com/pages/catering) for customers planning meals away from the property.

That broadens the business beyond a transaction at a meat counter. It gives families, organizations, and companies another place to meet and another local vendor to hire.

### It invests in public community events

On July 3, 2026, [KLTV and KSLA reported](https://www.kltv.com/2026/07/04/bear-creek-smokehouse-hosting-freedom-fireworks-show-marshall/) that Bear Creek was hosting its annual Freedom & Fireworks event with free parking and admission. The report said the tradition began in 2021 as a way to bring families and the community together.

The article also reported that prior events had drawn as many as 3,000 attendees. That number is an estimate attributed to the owner in local reporting, not an independently audited attendance count.

The broader point is verifiable: the business used its property, planning, staff time, and visibility to host a free public gathering.

## A practical visitor and customer guide

Bear Creek serves several different kinds of customers. Knowing which part of the business you need can prevent a wasted trip.

### For an in-person visit

The [official site currently lists](https://bearcreeksmokehouse.com/pages/about-us):

- **General Store:** Monday through Friday, 9 a.m.–5 p.m.; Saturday, 9 a.m.–6 p.m.; closed Sunday.
- **Kitchen:** Monday through Friday, 11 a.m.–5 p.m.; Saturday, 11 a.m.–6 p.m.
- **The Pit:** Friday, 11 a.m.–5 p.m.; Saturday, 11 a.m.–6 p.m.; or until sold out.
- **Telephone:** 903-935-5217.

Hours, menu availability, event schedules, and sellout times can change. Call or check the official site before making a long drive, especially for pit service or a specific product.

### For shipped food or gifts

The official online store offers individual products, assortments, and gift items. Before ordering:

1. confirm the recipient’s complete address and telephone number;
2. review the current shipping notice and delivery estimate;
3. check storage and heating instructions for the specific product;
4. order early for holidays or time-sensitive gifts; and
5. contact the business directly about large or corporate orders.

Perishable-food delivery can be affected by weather and carrier delays. A promised dinner date is not the time to discover that an address or arrival window was wrong.

### For catering or events

Use the official catering and venue pages as a starting point, then confirm the current menu, pricing, capacity, available dates, cleaning charges, setup rules, and cancellation policy directly with the business.

Do not assume a public festival schedule or ordinary lunch service means the venue is automatically available for a private event.

## Lessons other family businesses can use

Bear Creek’s public history offers several lessons that apply beyond food.

### Keep the core product recognizable

Expansion works better when customers can still explain what the business is known for. Bear Creek added retail, dining, events, catering, and online ordering around its smoked-food identity.

### Build more than one path to the customer

A rural business cannot rely only on passing traffic. In-person shopping, food service, shipped orders, bulk gifts, catering, and events create multiple reasons for customers to engage.

### Make the location part of the experience

The Piney Woods setting is not treated as a disadvantage to hide. The store, porch, animals, food, and event space make the trip part of what customers are buying.

### Document the family story accurately

“Family-owned” means more when the names, dates, founders, and leadership transitions are preserved. Bear Creek repeatedly identifies Hick and Nellie, the 1943 beginning, and the later generations carrying the operation.

### Community involvement should be concrete

The strongest community claims are specific: an event was held, admission was free, a venue was opened, or a service was provided. Those details are more meaningful than a vague claim that a company “cares.”

## Editorial perspective

Bear Creek Smokehouse is worth studying because its growth is rooted in something understandable.

A family began with a practical product. Later generations kept the product, survived disruptions, expanded the ways customers could buy it, and turned a rural property into a destination.

The result is bigger than a plate of barbecue. It is a working example of succession, adaptation, place-based marketing, and community participation.

East Texas needs new companies. It also needs established local businesses that keep reinvesting, transferring knowledge, and giving the next generation something real to carry forward.
