Longview’s Christmas parade is months away.

The work to get into it starts today.

According to the [official Downtown Longview AMBUCS Christmas Parade page](https://www.visitlongviewtexas.com/268/Christmas-Parade), applications for the 2026 parade open September 5. The listed application fee is $50, space is limited, and submitting an application does not guarantee a place in the parade.

The theme is **A Texas Christmas**.

That gives schools, churches, businesses, clubs, civic groups, and neighborhood organizations a clear prompt. It also gives them a reason not to wait until the last minute.

{{callout: key | An application is not a reservation. Longview says space is limited and submission does not guarantee participation.}}

## What the official page confirms

The city’s visitor bureau page lists four details that matter before a group starts buying decorations:

- the application period opens September 5, 2026
- the application fee is $50
- space is limited
- an application does not guarantee participation

The page also says entries must show a decorative Christmas spirit through their floats, vehicles, and costumes. The 2026 theme is A Texas Christmas.

Those are the confirmed facts available when this article was verified at 3:42 a.m. Central on September 5.

The page marks the opening date as subject to change. Applicants should recheck the official page before submitting and should treat the application itself as the controlling document for deadlines, dimensions, safety rules, and lineup requirements.

## Who should consider applying

A parade entry can make sense for more than a business with a large float budget.

Schools can build around band, athletics, career programs, or campus traditions. Churches and service groups can highlight volunteer work. Local businesses can recognize employees instead of turning the entry into a rolling advertisement. Clubs can use the theme to show something distinctly East Texas.

The strongest idea is usually the one a group can execute safely and clearly.

A smaller, finished entry is better than a complicated concept that cannot survive a December evening, a long staging period, or a moving route.

## Use this application checklist

Before one person submits on behalf of a group, settle these questions:

1. **Who owns the application?** Name one person responsible for notices, payment, and follow-up.
2. **What is the entry type?** Float, vehicle, walking group, performance unit, or another format allowed by the official application.
3. **How does it fit A Texas Christmas?** Write the concept in one sentence.
4. **Who is responsible for safety?** Account for riders, walkers, lighting, power, weather, and movement.
5. **What is the real budget?** Include the $50 application fee plus materials, transportation, storage, and cleanup.
6. **What happens if the group is not selected?** Decide whether decorations can be reused at another event.

Do not collect money or promise participants a place until the organizer confirms acceptance.

## What remains uncertain

The public information available before publication does not answer every operational question a participant may have.

Groups still need to confirm the final submission deadline, acceptance timeline, staging instructions, route rules, vehicle requirements, and any restrictions on sound, throwing items, animals, generators, or lighting. Those details may appear in the application or later participant instructions.

The official page provides a public contact route for questions through the Longview Convention and Visitors Bureau. It lists 903-753-3281 and info@VisitLongviewTexas.com.

Ask before building around an assumption.

## Make the entry useful after parade night

A parade can create one good night. The work can create more than that.

Photograph the build process. Save the supply list. Record the names of volunteers who want to help again. Design signs and decorations that can be reused. If the entry represents a service organization, give spectators one simple way to learn about the work after the parade.

That turns a seasonal project into a reusable community asset.

## The next move is simple

If your group wants to participate, read the [official parade page](https://www.visitlongviewtexas.com/268/Christmas-Parade), open the application when it is available, and assign one person to own the process.

Then build around the theme you can actually finish.

Texas Christmas can be big. It can also be local, handmade, and honest.

The application starts the process. The follow-through makes the entry.
