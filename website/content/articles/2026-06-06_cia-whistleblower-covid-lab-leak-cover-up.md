On May 13, 2026, a CIA senior operations officer named James Erdman III was sworn in before the Senate Homeland Security Committee.

Under oath.

On camera.

He testified that internal CIA analysts determined as early as 2021 that COVID-19 most likely originated from a leak at the Wuhan Institute of Virology — and that senior Biden administration officials subsequently altered and suppressed that finding to push a different story.

Not a theory. A sworn testimony.

And the Democratic members of the Senate Homeland Security Committee did not show up to hear it.

## What Erdman Actually Said

James Erdman isn't a political operative or a media personality. He is — or was — a career intelligence officer. He co-founded Feds For Freedom during the COVID vaccine mandate battles, which tells you something about his willingness to take on institutional pressure, but his testimony was not ideological noise. It was a direct account of what he witnessed inside the CIA.

According to his testimony and reporting from the Daily Caller, Fox News, and C-SPAN's full hearing record:

- An internal CIA investigative group reached the conclusion that COVID originated from a Wuhan lab leak.
- That conclusion, assigned a high probability, was then reworked by more senior officials to downgrade the lab leak finding and elevate the natural transmission theory.
- The revision aligned with the positions held by a "tight-knit group of virologists close to Anthony Fauci."
- The final public-facing intelligence assessment buried what the agency's own biological experts had concluded.

The C-SPAN video is publicly available. The Senate testimony is on record. This is not a rumor. It is a sworn account from someone who was there.

## The Empty Chairs Tell the Whole Story

I want to be precise here because the political optics matter: no Democratic members of the Senate Homeland Security Committee appeared for Erdman's testimony.

Not a single one.

There are a few ways to interpret that. You could say it was scheduling. You could say it was a protest against what they viewed as a partisan framing. You could say they didn't believe the testimony was credible.

But here's what I know from experience: when you don't want something on the record, you stay out of the room.

That absence is its own kind of receipt.
