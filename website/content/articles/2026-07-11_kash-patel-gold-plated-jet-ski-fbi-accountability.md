On July 10, the sitting Director of the FBI responded to a column questioning his use of a $60 million taxpayer-funded FBI jet — flights that reportedly included trips to see his girlfriend perform — by posting this:

https://x.com/Kash_Patel/status/2075544869267706152

"Nah, my jet ski is gold plated…. dumbass."

That's it. That's the answer the American public got from the man running the agency that spent years telling this country it needed warrants at dawn, SWAT teams at doors, and years in prison to hold ordinary people "accountable."

I want to be careful here, because this site runs on documents, not vibes. So let's separate what's confirmed from what isn't.

## What's actually on the record

Senate Judiciary Chairman Chuck Grassley sent Director Patel a formal letter asking him to account for his personal use of FBI aircraft, including the travel described above, and asking why Patel has reportedly favored BMWs over the standard-issue Chevy Suburban for his security detail. That letter is public. Patel's response — the "gold plated" line — is his own post, on his own account, in his own words. I'm not characterizing it. You can read it yourself above.

What I *am* doing is telling you what it looks like from where I sit.

## I know what FBI "accountability" looks like when it's aimed at you

I've lived on the other side of an FBI investigation. I know what it is to have federal agents build a case around every hour of your day, every dollar you spent, every text you sent. I know what it is to have prosecutors stand up in court and argue that the details — the small stuff, the stuff that looks like nothing — matter. That they show a "pattern." That they prove something about your character.

So when the man at the top of that same institution gets asked a direct question about how *he* spent public money, and the answer is a joke about a jet ski, I don't think that's nothing. I think it's the pattern. It's just running in the other direction now.

I'm not writing this because I think Kash Patel is uniquely dishonest or uniquely reckless. I'm writing this because the reaction — deflect, joke, move on — is exactly the reaction the FBI has never once extended to people like me. The bar for "explain yourself" apparently depends entirely on which side of the badge you're standing on.

## The test doesn't change based on who's in the chair

I said it in the piece I wrote about the January 6 surveillance footage, and I'll say it again here: if you have nothing to hide, showing your work costs you nothing. Release the flight logs. Itemize the spending. Answer Grassley's letter line by line, on the record, instead of a one-liner that treats a Senate oversight request like a heckler at a rally.

That standard should apply to me, to you, to the guy in a MAGA hat who got sentenced two years past what his own conduct justified — and it should apply to the FBI Director too. Especially to the FBI Director.
