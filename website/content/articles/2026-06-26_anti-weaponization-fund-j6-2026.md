A $1.776 billion federal fund is being created for Americans who say they were politically targeted by the government.

That is the headline. Here is my problem with it.

I am one of those Americans. I was arrested, charged, and held as a pretrial detainee in conditions that a federal judge later described as punitive. I have documented what happened to me. And I need to say something about this fund that I suspect is not going to be popular in every corner of my corner of the internet.

## What the Fund Actually Is

The DOJ created the Anti-Weaponization Fund as part of a settlement after President Trump and members of his family sued the IRS over alleged political targeting. The fund totals nearly $1.8 billion. The mechanism is the Federal Tort Claims Act — meaning individuals can file administrative claims asserting government misconduct and potentially receive payouts.

Applications have already started rolling in, even though the official process has not yet launched.

NBC News reported the scramble is already underway:

https://x.com/NBCNews/status/2057768458092220717

But here is where it gets complicated.

Acting AG Todd Blanche publicly said the fund "will not be moving forward." The DOJ told a federal court it was dead. And yet — administration insiders are quietly assuring allies that some form of payout remains on track.

Meet the Press caught that tension:

https://x.com/MeetThePress/status/2061810263582458306

So which is it? Dead or alive?

## The Part That Bothers Me

I spent years watching the media paint every January 6 defendant with the same brush. We were all insurrectionists. All terrorists. No distinction between the person who threw a fire extinguisher and the person — like me — who walked in, got confronted, and became the government's case study.

I am not going to do the same thing in reverse.

Not every J6 defendant suffered equally. Not every claim filed against this fund will be legitimate. And a man in South Carolina who is charging fellow J6 defendants a ten percent cut — capped at five thousand dollars apiece — to help them file FTCA claims is not the hero of this story. He is a middleman extracting a fee from people whose cases he does not understand and whose suffering he did not share.

I am wary of any process that does not distinguish between people who were genuinely harmed by a politically motivated prosecution and people who are rushing to cash in because the political winds shifted.

I was harmed. I can document that. But that documentation does not make every claim that walks through the door equally valid.

## What Compensation Can and Cannot Do

There is something the money cannot buy back.

It cannot buy back the relationships that broke while I was locked up. It cannot give me back the years I spent under the weight of federal charges. It cannot undo what I watched happen to other defendants — the ones who went into pretrial detention healthy and came out broken.

I am not anti-compensation. If the government targeted people illegally, those people deserve to have the record corrected and the harm acknowledged. That is what real accountability looks like.

But a $1.776 billion fund pushed through under political pressure, announced with a settlement, publicly declared dead by the AG, and then quietly kept alive by insiders is not accountability. It is political management. An administration doing something because it owed something to a constituency, then running from the fallout when Senate Republicans pushed back hard.

I want the real thing. I want the government to acknowledge, on the record, what it did — not pay out quietly and move on.

## My Honest Position

I am not filing a claim through this fund yet. I am watching how the process develops. I want to see whether it produces actual acknowledgment of actual harm, or whether it becomes a payout mechanism that lets everyone declare victory without doing the hard work of proving which specific cases deserved which specific remedies.

The J6 community is not monolithic. The harm was not uniform. Any fund that pretends otherwise is not actually serving the people who need it most.

The people who need it most are the ones who can prove what happened to them — with documentation, with records, with receipts. That is the standard I hold myself to. It should be the standard for everyone.
