A seventeen-year-old should not have to understand every possible future before graduation.

They should get a real chance to compare the doors.

Longview’s College and Career Expo is scheduled for Thursday, October 1, from 9 a.m. to 2 p.m. at Maude Cobb Convention Center, 100 Grande Boulevard. The City of Longview says the event is designed to connect high school juniors and seniors across Region 7 and Region 8 school districts with colleges, universities, trade schools, military service, and employers.

The city posted the current event notice on September 28. Details were verified against that official notice on September 30, 2026.

## Who the expo is built for

The [official City of Longview announcement](https://www.longviewtexas.gov/m/newsflash/Home/Detail/6558) speaks directly to area schools, counselors, vendors, and organizations that can show students what comes after graduation.

The intended student audience is high school juniors and seniors attending through participating Region 7 and Region 8 school districts.

That distinction matters. The city’s notice does not clearly advertise the event as a general public walk-in fair. A parent or student who is not attending with a school should contact the organizer or school counselor before driving to the convention center.

What is confirmed:

- Thursday, October 1;
- 9 a.m. to 2 p.m.;
- Maude Cobb Convention Center;
- colleges, universities, trade schools, military service, and career fields; and
- participating schools serving juniors and seniors.

What remains uncertain from the public notice is the final vendor list, the exact student schedule, and whether independent walk-ins will be admitted.

## Why one room can matter

Career advice often arrives as a slogan.

Go to college. Learn a trade. Join the military. Start working. Build a business.

Each path can be right for somebody. None should be sold as the automatic answer for everybody.

A useful expo lets students ask practical questions before a choice becomes an application, a loan, a contract, or a move away from home.

For a college or university:

- What does the full first year cost after ordinary aid?
- Which programs lead to a specific license or credential?
- What percentage of students finish the program?
- Can credits transfer?

For a trade school:

- How long is training?
- What tools, exams, and fees are not included in tuition?
- Is the credential recognized by Texas employers?
- Is apprenticeship placement available?

For an employer:

- What entry-level roles are open to a new graduate?
- Is paid training available?
- What schedule, travel, or physical demands come with the work?
- What can somebody earn after one, three, and five years?

For military service:

- What is guaranteed in writing?
- Which job field is attached to the contract?
- What are the service obligation and reserve commitment?
- Which education and credential benefits apply?

The goal is not to find the table with the best giveaway. It is to leave with comparable facts.

## A five-question student plan

Students can make the day more useful by bringing five questions on paper or in a phone note:

1. What does this path cost me in money and time?
2. What credential, skill, or experience do I have when I finish?
3. What is the first realistic job afterward?
4. What is the biggest reason people quit or fail?
5. What should I do in the next thirty days if I am interested?

Take a photo of each table’s contact information. Write one sentence beside it before moving on. By the end, every option should have a next step.

## What parents and counselors can do today

If your student is in Region 7 or Region 8, ask the school counselor whether the campus is attending and what permission or transportation plan applies.

If you represent a school, college, training provider, military branch, or employer, use the registration links in the city’s official announcement and confirm availability directly with the organizer.

Share this with a family that has a junior or senior. The deadline may not be a form on a government page. It may be the moment the bus leaves tomorrow morning.
