The Department of Justice has opened a criminal probe involving E. Jean Carroll's lawsuits against President Donald Trump.

The investigation — focused on a trust founded by LinkedIn billionaire and Democratic donor Reid Hoffman, whose nonprofit helped fund Carroll's legal costs — is examining potential money laundering, obstruction, and conspiracy.

[CNN broke the story](https://www.cnn.com/2026/05/27/politics/exclusive-justice-department-launched-e-jean-carroll-investigation). [The Washington Post confirmed the probe's focus on Hoffman's nonprofit](https://www.washingtonpost.com/national-security/2026/05/28/doj-probes-reid-hoffmans-nonprofit-funding-e-jean-carrolls-legal-bills/).

Then something strange happened.

U.S. Attorney Andrew Boutros — a Trump appointee — publicly stated that the Northern District of Illinois "has not opened — and has never opened — a criminal investigation into E. Jean Carroll." One day after CNN published the original story.

CNN's sources reaffirmed the investigation to them after Boutros' denial.

And X has been arguing about it ever since.

## The Legal Theory Behind the Investigation

Here is what the DOJ is reportedly basing this on.

In a 2022 deposition, Carroll stated she had not received outside funding for her lawsuit against Trump. Reid Hoffman was later revealed to have helped fund Carroll's legal battle through a nonprofit he backed. Prosecutors are now examining whether that statement — "I have not received outside funding" — constitutes a material misrepresentation, and whether the funding arrangement constitutes obstruction or conspiracy.

[Time magazine reported on the investigation](https://time.com/article/2026/05/28/e-jean-carroll-trump-justice-department-criminal-investigation/) and noted the probe was opened by U.S. Attorney Andrew Boutros, who was appointed to his position by Trump.

That last detail is not a footnote. It is the center of the story.

The sitting President's own appointee is overseeing an investigation into the funding sources of the sitting President's most prominent accuser. That is an extraordinary situation by any measure.

## Reid Hoffman's Response

Hoffman did not stay quiet.

[In a statement to CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/05/29/politics/reid-hoffman-e-jean-carroll-probe-nonprofit-trump-critics), Hoffman said the Justice Department's criminal probe is designed to "silence" those who stand up to President Trump.

That is a direct accusation that the DOJ is being weaponized against political opponents and critics.

It is also the kind of accusation that, if true, would represent one of the most serious abuses of executive power in modern American history.

Here is where I have to be honest with you about how I think about this.

I have been on the receiving end of what it looks like when systems are used to target people. I have documented coordinated harassment, platform censorship, and legal pressure used as a tool to silence voices. I have written about it here, with receipts.

So I do not dismiss "this is being used to silence me" as a reflexive talking point. I know what that dynamic can look like.

And I also know what it looks like when powerful people use "I'm being silenced" as a shield against legitimate accountability.

The question with Hoffman and Carroll is: which of those things is this?

## The Arguments X Is Making — Both Sides

The case for the investigation being legitimate:

If Carroll stated under oath that she had not received outside funding, and she had in fact received it, that is a perjury problem. Funding source transparency in litigation matters. Courts rely on parties to disclose their resources, particularly in cases involving financial damages and public figures. If Hoffman's nonprofit was deliberately structured to hide its involvement in the litigation, that raises real legal questions.

The case for the investigation being retaliation:

A Trump-appointed U.S. Attorney is investigating the funding sources of a woman who successfully sued Donald Trump for sexual abuse and defamation. The probe was opened after Trump returned to office. Carroll's original deposition statement about funding was made in 2022, when the DOJ was under Biden. It took until Trump's second term for it to be characterized as potentially criminal. The timing is not subtle.

[NBC News laid out both dimensions carefully](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/justice-department/doj-opens-criminal-probe-trump-accuser-e-jean-carroll-rcna347257).

## The Contradiction That Should Bother Everyone

There is a contradiction at the center of this story that I do not think gets enough attention.

The right, broadly speaking, spent years arguing that the DOJ had been weaponized under Biden — that the political prosecution of Trump, the January 6 cases, and the FBI's conduct were examples of a captured law enforcement apparatus serving the president's interests.

I have agreed with parts of that argument. I have documented what I believe was selective enforcement and political targeting in my own case.

But the logical consequence of believing that DOJ weaponization is wrong — regardless of which administration does it — is that you have to apply that standard consistently.

If a Trump DOJ opens a criminal probe into Trump's accuser, and the timing and target of that probe look politically motivated, you have to ask the same questions you asked about Biden's DOJ.

You do not get to object to DOJ weaponization only when it runs against your preferred president.

Either law enforcement independence matters or it does not. Pick a lane and stay in it.

## What the Record Shows

Here is what I know to be true right now.

Carroll won her civil cases. The jury found in her favor. Trump was found liable.

The DOJ is now investigating the funding sources behind Carroll's legal effort. The investigation was opened by a Trump appointee. The target of those lawsuits is the current President of the United States.

The U.S. Attorney who reportedly opened the probe publicly denied it — while sources close to the investigation reaffirmed it to CNN.

There is a contradiction in the public record that has not been resolved.

And the question of whether a sitting President's Justice Department should be investigating the funding of litigation brought against that same President is not a question that should be answered by which team you're on.

It is a structural question about whether the DOJ can function as an independent institution when the subject of the litigation is the man who controls its budget and its personnel.

That question matters.

Watch how this develops. The story is not over.
