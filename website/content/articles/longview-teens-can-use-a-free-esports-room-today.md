Longview families looking for a free, supervised place where a teenager can spend part of Friday afternoon have a practical option today.

The City of Longview has scheduled a teen esports game day at Broughton Recreation Center from 4:15 to 5:45 p.m. on Friday, September 4, 2026.

The program is for ages 13 through 17. It is free, and a recreation center membership is not required.

That is the useful headline. The next step is to verify the details before heading out.

## What the city says is available

The [official Longview calendar listing](https://longviewtexas.gov/Calendar.aspx?EID=10885) says the center's esports game room has high-performance computers and gaming consoles. The program is designed to give teens time to play with peers and develop social skills.

The listed location is:

**Broughton Recreation Center**  
**801 S. Martin Luther King Jr. Blvd.**  
**Longview, Texas 75602**

The scheduled time is **4:15 to 5:45 p.m.**

The city lists the activity for teens **13 to 17 years old** and says no membership is needed.

For questions, the calendar provides a contact number of **903-237-1345** and the email **JClay@longviewtexas.gov**.

Families should contact the center before leaving if capacity, transportation, accessibility, equipment availability, or a last-minute schedule change could affect the trip. An online calendar is a strong starting point, but it cannot promise that every station will be open when a particular teen arrives.

## Why a small Friday program matters

Not every public-value story needs a giant project or a heated meeting.

Sometimes the value is a room, a time, and a clear invitation.

The hour after school can be difficult to fill. Some families are working. Some teens do not play organized sports. Some cannot afford a commercial gaming setup or a paid club. Some simply want a structured place to meet people who enjoy the same thing.

An esports room is not a replacement for every kind of youth program. It is one more door.

That matters because participation often begins with fit. A teenager who will not attend a formal workshop may show up for a game. Once there, the useful part can extend beyond the screen: learning to share equipment, handle frustration, communicate with teammates, and return to a public space with expectations.

Those outcomes depend on how the program is run. The city listing describes the purpose, not a measured result for every participant. Families should judge the actual experience for themselves.

## What is known and what remains uncertain

As verified on September 4, the official listing establishes the date, time, address, age range, free admission, lack of a membership requirement, and the general equipment available.

The listing does not state:

- how many gaming stations are available
- whether advance registration is required
- which games are offered today
- whether a parent must remain on site
- how equipment is assigned if turnout exceeds capacity
- whether food, drinks, or outside controllers are permitted

Those are good questions for the center. Do not assume an answer from a promotional sentence.

The city's calendar also says the activity is planned for Fridays, with special dates pending. That suggests a recurring opportunity, but families should verify each date instead of treating today's listing as a permanent weekly guarantee.

## A four-question check before you go

Call the center and ask:

1. Is today's session still running from 4:15 to 5:45?
2. Does a teen need to register before arriving?
3. What does the teen need to bring for check-in?
4. Is there a waitlist or capacity limit?

If the answers work for your family, save the address and arrive early enough to handle check-in without losing most of the session.

For families seeking other programs, the city maintains a [Teen Activities page](https://www.longviewtexas.gov/4404/Teen-Activities). The [Broughton Branch Library page](https://longviewtexas.gov/2892/Broughton-Branch-Library) is another place to check for public resources at the same community complex.
