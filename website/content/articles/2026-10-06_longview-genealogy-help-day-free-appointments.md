Sometimes the missing piece of a family story is sitting in a newspaper nobody has opened in decades.

Sometimes it is a name spelled three different ways.

Sometimes it is not missing at all. You just need somebody who knows which drawer, database, reel, or record to check next.

The Longview Public Library is offering that kind of help today.

The city’s official calendar lists Genealogy Help Day for Tuesday, October 6, 2026, from 3:00 to 6:00 p.m. at the Longview Public Library, 222 W. Cotton St. The event is free, but the library asks people to reserve a 30-minute or one-hour appointment.

The details in this article were verified against the City of Longview event page at 4:18 a.m. Central on October 6, 2026.

## What the library says volunteers can help with

The official [Genealogy Help Day listing](https://www.longviewtexas.gov/Calendar.aspx?EID=10971&calType=0&day=6&month=10&year=2026) describes an open workshop for family-tree research, old newspaper searches on the library’s microfilm equipment, Texas history papers, and basic questions from people who are not sure how genealogy research works.

The help is scheduled to come from the library’s genealogy librarian and volunteers with the Gregg County Genealogical Society.

That combination matters. A database search can produce hundreds of possible matches. A local researcher may recognize a church name, cemetery, neighborhood, newspaper title, county boundary, or record-keeping habit that helps narrow the search.

The event is useful for experienced researchers who have hit a wall. It is also built for beginners who have nothing more than a name and a question.

## Who is likely to benefit

The obvious audience is somebody building a family tree.

The opportunity is broader than that.

A student writing about Texas history may need help locating a contemporary newspaper account. A homeowner may be curious about an old address. A church, nonprofit, or neighborhood group may be trying to identify people in an undated photograph. A family may want to preserve names and relationships before the people who remember them are gone.

Genealogy work can also correct confident family stories that turned out to be incomplete. That does not make the old story worthless. It gives the next generation a chance to separate memory, evidence, and unanswered questions.

The library’s event does not promise that every question will be solved. It offers something more realistic: a trained person, a defined block of time, and access to tools that many residents do not have at home.

## What to bring without bringing too much

Good research starts with a narrow question.

Instead of arriving with “Find my whole family,” try one of these:

- Where was this person living in 1940?
- Can we identify the newspaper that covered this event?
- Which county would have held this record at the time?
- Is this a maiden name, a middle name, or a spelling change?
- What record should I search after the census trail stops?

Bring the names, approximate dates, and places you already know. A handwritten list is enough. If you have copies of public records or newspaper clippings, organize only the few that relate to the question.

Do not carry original irreplaceable documents unless the library specifically asks for them. Do not place Social Security numbers, current account information, medical records, or living relatives’ private details into a public research file. Family history can be meaningful without exposing information that does not belong in the room.

The same privacy principle applies online. [A good intake form asks what must stay private](/posts/good-intake-form-asks-what-must-stay-private) explains why gathering information and having permission to share it are different things.

## How to reserve time

The city listing says appointments can be arranged by calling **903-237-1351**. It also lists **Cpippins@longviewtexas.gov** and the Longview Public Library’s Facebook page as contact options.

Because the event uses 30-minute and one-hour blocks, do not assume a walk-in opening will be available. Call before going.

Ask three practical questions:

1. Is an appointment still open today?
2. Should I bring a library card or identification for the resource I want to use?
3. Can the library help with my specific record type, or should I begin somewhere else?

The official listing says the workshop is free. It does not state that copies, printing, outside database services, or later record orders are free. Ask about any possible cost before requesting materials.

## What is known and what remains uncertain

Here is what the city page confirms:

- The event is Tuesday, October 6, 2026.
- The hours are 3:00 to 6:00 p.m.
- The location is Longview Public Library, 222 W. Cotton St.
- The workshop is free.
- Appointments are offered in 30-minute or one-hour blocks.
- A genealogy librarian and Gregg County Genealogical Society volunteers are expected to assist.

The listing does not publish the number of available appointments, the exact databases that will be open to each visitor, or whether every volunteer will be able to work with every record type. Availability may change during the day.

That is why the call matters.

## Leave with a research trail, not just an answer

If a librarian or volunteer finds something useful, write down where it came from.

Record the newspaper title, date, page number, reel, database, collection name, county office, or catalog entry. A fact without its source becomes difficult to verify later.

If the search comes up empty, record that too. Note the spelling, date range, and collection searched. “Nothing found” in one index does not mean the event never happened. It means the next search should begin somewhere different.

That habit is useful in genealogy, public records, business research, and any story that matters. [An edit date is not a verification date](/posts/an-edit-date-is-not-a-verification-date) explains why the trail needs the moment of checking, not only the moment a file was changed.

Longview is offering residents a small, practical doorway into that work today.

If you have one family-history question you keep carrying, reserve the time, narrow the question, and bring what you know.

What name, place, photograph, or old story would you ask the library to help you trace?
