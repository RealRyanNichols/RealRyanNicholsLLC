If you planned to burn brush, yard debris, or household waste in Gregg County today, stop before you strike the match.

The [official Gregg County website](https://greggcounty.texas.gov/) currently displays “Burn Ban is in Effect.” That is the clearest public signal available from the county on the morning of October 1, 2026.

It is also the beginning of the answer, not the entire answer.

Texas burn restrictions depend on the local order, whether the property is inside a city, what is being burned, and whether another law already prohibits the activity. Residents need to verify the rule that applies to the exact location before lighting anything outdoors.

## What is confirmed

Gregg County’s home page links to a county graphic stating that the burn ban is in effect. The county has not placed the full order, its adoption date, or a list of exceptions in the text of that home-page notice.

Texas law gives a county commissioners court authority to restrict outdoor burning in all or part of the county’s unincorporated area when drought conditions exist or when officials find that outdoor burning would worsen a public-safety hazard. That authority appears in [Section 352.081 of the Texas Local Government Code](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/LG/pdf/LG.352.pdf).

The location matters. A county order generally addresses unincorporated territory. A property inside Longview, Kilgore, Gladewater, White Oak, Clarksville City, Lakeport, or another municipality may also be governed by city rules.

## What the state guidance says

The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality explains that outdoor burning is generally prohibited unless a state rule provides an exception. Even when an exception would normally allow a fire, a local ordinance or burn ban can add another restriction.

TCEQ’s current guide, [Outdoor Burning in Texas](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/goto/rg-049), says county judges and commissioners courts may issue burn bans for unincorporated parts of a county. It also says TCEQ does not issue or administer those local bans.

That division of responsibility is important. Calling TCEQ may help with state outdoor-burning rules, but the county or city is the source for the local restriction in force today.

## Who is affected

The notice matters most to people planning any open fire in unincorporated Gregg County, including landowners, contractors, ranchers, and residents clearing storm or yard debris.

It also matters to people who assume a small pile is harmless.

Wind can move embers beyond a fence line. Dry grass can carry fire farther than the original pile. A legal fire can become an illegal or dangerous one if it escapes control. TCEQ warns that people responsible for escaped fires may face criminal penalties or civil claims for the damage.

This is not a reason to panic. It is a reason to check before acting.

## What remains uncertain from the county notice

The county’s public home-page notice does not answer several practical questions:

- the exact geographic coverage of the current order;
- the date the order began;
- when commissioners will review or end it;
- whether the order names specific exceptions; and
- which county office should provide a copy of the signed order.

Those details should not be guessed.

Conditions can change after rain, but a rain shower does not automatically cancel a formal order. A ban remains a legal restriction until the authorized local action changes it.

## What to do before any outdoor fire

Use this short sequence.

### 1. Confirm whether the property is inside a city

Do not rely on a mailing address alone. A Longview postal address can still sit outside Longview city limits.

### 2. Check the county’s current notice

Start with the [Gregg County home page](https://greggcounty.texas.gov/). Save the county’s main number, 903-758-6181, if the public notice does not answer your question.

### 3. Ask for the actual order

Ask the county or the applicable municipal fire authority for the signed order or ordinance, its effective date, and any written exceptions. Do not treat a social-media comment as a substitute.

### 4. Check the state rule too

Read the [TCEQ outdoor-burning guide](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/goto/rg-049) for the baseline Texas rules. Local permission does not make a fire lawful if state rules prohibit the material or method.

### 5. Use another disposal option

Delay the burn, chip or haul brush, use an authorized waste service, or ask local officials about permitted disposal sites. The safest fire during an active ban is the one that never starts.
