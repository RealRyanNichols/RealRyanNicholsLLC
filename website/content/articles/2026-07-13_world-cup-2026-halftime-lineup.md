The FIFA World Cup has never had a halftime show. Not once, in over ninety years of finals.

That changes on July 19 at MetLife Stadium in New Jersey, and FIFA didn't ease into the idea — they went and booked Madonna, Shakira, BTS, and Justin Bieber to co-headline it, curated by Coldplay's Chris Martin, with Burna Boy, Gustavo Dudamel, the viral PS22 school choir, and characters from Sesame Street and The Muppets all sharing an eleven-minute runtime.

Eleven minutes. Five A-list acts (six counting Coldplay). Half the planet expected to watch.

## The Pitch Itself Was Perfect

Before any of this was official, FIFA released a teaser with Elmo and Chris Martin workshopping the lineup — Elmo suggesting Madonna, Cookie Monster pitching BTS, Animal throwing out Shakira. It's a genuinely funny bit, and it tells you exactly what FIFA is going for here: **Super Bowl halftime show, but for a billion more people, with a $100 million philanthropic hook attached.**

The show is funding the FIFA Global Citizen Education Fund, aiming to raise over $100 million to expand access to soccer and education for kids worldwide. Say what you want about corporate spectacle — that part is real money going somewhere real.

## Can Five Headliners Actually Fit in Eleven Minutes?

Here's my actual concern, and it's not a cynical one: the Super Bowl halftime show runs about 12-13 minutes and it's usually built around *one* artist's catalog, maybe two. FIFA just tried to fit five distinct global fanbases — Madonna's, Shakira's, BTS's, Bieber's, and Burna Boy's — into a runtime that's actually shorter.

That's not a show. That's a highlight reel of a show. Each act might get ninety seconds of an actual song before the transition hits. If FIFA pulls off making that feel like a cohesive moment instead of five ringtones stitched together, it'll be one of the great live-TV directing jobs of the decade. If they don't, it'll be the most star-studded fast-forward button in Halftime show history.

Chris Martin curating it is the right call, for what it's worth — Coldplay has built a career out of stadium-scale, everyone-sing-along spectacle, and "all about togetherness, everyone's invited" is exactly the tone this needed instead of five competing egos fighting for the same four minutes.

## Why This Matters Beyond the Music

Soccer in America has spent decades trying to convince casual fans it's must-watch programming, not background noise during a barbecue. Pairing the actual World Cup Final — first 48-team tournament ever, first final on U.S. soil since 1994 — with a halftime show built explicitly to pull in people who don't care about the sport is the smartest thing FIFA has done all tournament.

You don't get Madonna and BTS fans to sit through 90 minutes of football by accident. You get them there by giving them a reason that has nothing to do with football at all, and hoping the match hooks them once they're already on the couch.
