On July 9, Senate Judiciary Chairman Chuck Grassley posted this, in the middle of the fight over FBI Director Kash Patel's use of a taxpayer-funded jet:

https://x.com/ChuckGrassley/status/2075353795081408952

"I see Dems r riding my coattails & suddenly showing interest in doing oversight of FBI aircraft. Where was that energy w Wray & other directors??? My oversight is the same no matter the admin UNLIKE Dems who only seem interested in oversight when it benefits them politically."

I'm not going to pretend I don't have a reaction to that sentence. I do. But not the one you'd expect.

## He's right. And he's not right enough.

Grassley's point is simple: oversight that only shows up when it's politically convenient isn't oversight, it's theater. I don't disagree with that. What I'd ask him — respectfully, and on the record, the way I try to ask everyone — is to finish the sentence.

Where was *that* energy while Christopher Wray's FBI was running the largest single prosecution effort in American history against January 6 defendants? Where was the demand for itemized answers when agents were assigned to crowd-control missions the Bureau's own rules didn't clearly authorize? Where was the letter asking why some cases were charged as felonies and others, with similar conduct, weren't? I lived through that gap in "energy." It wasn't abstract to me. It was years of my life.

Grassley's post is about jets and spending. Fair fight, worth having. But the deeper point — that oversight shouldn't be a light switch you flip on only when it's your opponent in charge — is one I've been making since before most of Congress was paying attention to my case at all.

## Consistency is the whole ballgame

Here's the standard I keep coming back to on this site: if scrutiny is only real when it's aimed at the other team, it was never about accountability. It was about who's winning.

That cuts against Democrats who ignored FBI conduct for four years and suddenly found their voice this week. It also cuts against anyone on my side of the aisle who wants full-throated oversight of Kash Patel's travel budget but goes quiet the moment someone asks for the same rigor applied to how January 6 defendants were charged, surveilled, and sentenced. You don't get to pick which half of the FBI's conduct deserves a hearing.

I'll say the same thing about my own case that I say about everyone else's: put the record out, all of it, and let it hold up or fall apart on its own. That's not a partisan ask. It's the only ask that survives contact with whoever's in power next.
