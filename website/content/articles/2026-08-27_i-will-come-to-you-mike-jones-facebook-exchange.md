I am publishing this because the full sequence matters.

People are free to disagree with me. They can challenge my wording, question my sources, defend an elected official, or tell me I am wrong. That is public debate.

What people should not have to accept is a public argument escalating into language such as **“I will come to you!!!”** and **“You better do your homework before you take this test!!!!”**

## How the exchange began

I posted about publicly reported allegations involving Marion County Judge Leward J. LaFleur. My Facebook wording was blunt and provocative. The screenshot below preserves exactly what I wrote, rather than sanitizing it after the fact.

Mike Jones then entered the comments and wrote:

> **“I want to see the Judgement of Conviction or a Certificate Court Disposition. If you can’t provide proof, why are y’all slandering? That’s like a bunch of high school BS”**

His position appeared to be that the allegations should not be discussed publicly without a conviction or formal court disposition.

## What is publicly reported about Judge LaFleur

This article is not declaring Leward LaFleur guilty of a crime.

Local reporting in April 2026 described allegations of unwanted or inappropriate sexual touching following a 2025 Halloween party. [KLTV reported that an adult and a teenager made allegations and that LaFleur denied both allegations](https://www.kltv.com/2026/04/21/marion-county-judge-denies-allegations-unwanted-touching-halloween-party/).

Additional reporting said the first allegation was treated as a Class C assault matter involving a minor, that Marion County recused itself, that the matter went to another jurisdiction, and that a second adult allegation was identified during the review. [KETK reporting republished by AOL described that procedural history](https://www.aol.com/news/marion-county-judge-pushes-back-212823586.html).

The [Marion County government website](https://www.co.marion.tx.us/page/marion.County.Judge) identifies Leward J. LaFleur as the county judge.

Those are the facts I can responsibly state from the public record: allegations were publicly reported, legal and law-enforcement review was discussed, and LaFleur publicly denied the allegations.

**An allegation is not a conviction. A denial does not erase the fact that an allegation was made. Both statements can be true at the same time.**

Mike responded that the document was not a conviction and characterized it as a press release containing accusations.

That did not answer what I had actually said. I had not told him there was a conviction.

Then, while insisting that accusations involving LaFleur should not be discussed without a conviction, Mike brought up an accusation involving me:

> **“Didn’t you pull a gun on a guy at church?”**

He did not attach the judgment of conviction or court disposition that he had just demanded from me.

## Why I am calling the behavior threatening and intimidating

People can insult me. They can say my question was unfair. They can demand documents, defend a judge, challenge my credibility, or tell me to leave their page.

But when someone moves from **“do your homework”** to **“I will come to you!!!”** and follows it with **“before you take this test!!!!”**, I am going to preserve it and describe how I understood it.

I understood those words as threatening and intimidating behavior.

I am not declaring that Mike Jones committed a criminal offense. That is a legal conclusion for law enforcement, prosecutors, and courts to evaluate under the full facts and applicable law.

I do not need to exaggerate his words. The screenshot shows exactly what was displayed. If he intended something harmless, he can explain what he meant.

The quote is enough for the public to evaluate.

## This is also about what happens when people challenge local power

The larger issue is not one ugly Facebook argument.

When allegations involve a sitting county judge during an election season, people in the community are allowed to ask what happened, what was investigated, where the matter went, what witnesses said, what documents exist, and whether the process was handled properly.

That does not mean declaring guilt before proof.

It means refusing to confuse **“not convicted”** with **“nobody may discuss the allegation.”**

It also means applying the same standard to everyone.

This kind of escalation is familiar to people who speak publicly about controversial political issues. The disagreement quickly stops being about the underlying record. It becomes personal attacks, accusations, attempts to shame the speaker into silence, and language meant to make the speaker wonder whether confrontation will move offline.

That should not be accepted as normal political participation.

People should be able to question public officials, criticize political ideologies, challenge local institutions, and ask difficult questions without being told that someone will “come to” them.
