Longview is offering a direct path from training to a possible seasonal job.

It is not a show-up-and-get-certified class.

The city says participants must first prove they can complete a continuous swim, tread water using only their legs, retrieve a weighted object from depth, return with it, and exit the pool without a ladder or steps.

The fall course begins Friday, October 2.

## What the city has posted

The [City of Longview Parks and Recreation lifeguard page](https://www.longviewtexas.gov/4215/Lifeguards-Needed) lists a fall class running from **Friday, October 2, through Sunday, October 11**. The posted cost is **$100**, and the page includes an online registration link.

The city describes it as an American Red Cross Lifeguarding course for people interested in future lifeguard positions.

Successful participants earn an American Red Cross Lifeguarding certification with CPR/AED for Professional Rescuers and First Aid. The city says the certification is valid for two years.

Details were verified on September 27, 2026. Registration availability and schedules can change, so confirm through the official page before making plans.

{{callout: key | Registration buys a place in the process. Passing the prerequisites is what allows a participant to continue into the course.}}

## The prerequisite test is specific

Participants must be at least **15 years old by the final day of the course**.

Before the main training begins, they must complete two Red Cross prerequisite skills.

The first is a swim-tread-swim sequence:

1. Swim 150 yards.
2. Tread water for two minutes using only the legs.
3. Swim another 50 yards without stopping.

The second is a timed rescue-style event. Within **one minute and 40 seconds**, the participant must:

1. swim 20 yards;
2. surface dive to a depth of seven to ten feet;
3. retrieve a 10-pound object;
4. return 20 yards on the back while holding the object at the surface; and
5. exit the water without using a ladder or steps.

The city states that a participant who does not complete the prerequisite skills cannot continue into the lifeguarding course.

## What the course teaches

According to the official description, training covers:

- recognizing and preventing aquatic emergencies;
- water rescues;
- surveillance;
- extrication;
- CPR/AED for professional rescuers;
- First Aid; and
- emergency response skills.

Those subjects matter because lifeguarding is not simply strong swimming. A lifeguard has to watch for trouble before it becomes obvious, enter the water safely, move another person, coordinate a response, and document incidents afterward.

The city's seasonal job description includes supervising swimmers, helping maintain pool facilities, and preparing accident and incident reports.

## The posted seasonal job starts at $15.24 an hour

The same city page lists the seasonal lifeguard position at **$15.24 per hour**.

It describes the role as entry level and says no experience is required. It does require the Red Cross lifeguard, First Aid, and CPR/AED professional rescuer certifications, along with a valid Texas driver's license.

Training does not guarantee employment. The page connects the course to future positions, but it does not promise that every successful participant will receive an offer, a specific schedule, or a set number of hours.

That distinction is worth asking about before registering.

## Questions to ask before paying

The official page gives the core facts, but it does not publish every logistical detail a family or working participant may need.

Call Longview Parks and Recreation at **903-237-1270** and ask:

- Which dates and hours require attendance?
- Where will the prerequisite test and remaining sessions be held?
- Is the $100 refundable if a participant does not pass the prerequisite test?
- What equipment should the participant bring?
- Are make-up sessions available?
- When are seasonal jobs normally posted and interviewed?
- Does the valid Texas driver's license requirement apply to every applicant, including a 15-year-old trainee?

That last question matters because the course age and the posted job requirement are not identical. The city page allows a participant who turns 15 by the final day to attempt the course, while the job description separately lists a driver's license.

Do not guess. Ask the city how it applies those requirements.

## A six-day preparation plan

Someone considering the class has only a few days to assess readiness. Do not attempt a deep-water retrieval alone. Use a staffed pool and follow pool rules.

A simple preparation check can look like this:

- **Day 1:** Confirm registration, schedule, refund policy, and location.
- **Day 2:** Test a relaxed 200-yard continuous swim at a supervised pool.
- **Day 3:** Practice a two-minute legs-only tread under supervision.
- **Day 4:** Ask qualified pool staff about safe surface-dive and object-retrieval practice.
- **Day 5:** Review CPR/AED course expectations and assemble required gear.
- **Day 6:** Rest, hydrate, and arrive ready to follow instructions.

The goal is not to cram fitness into six days. It is to learn whether the prerequisite is realistic and to avoid arriving surprised.
