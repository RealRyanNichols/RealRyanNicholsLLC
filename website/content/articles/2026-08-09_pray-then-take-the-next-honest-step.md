*By the Real Ryan Nichols Editorial Team*

Pray about it.

Then stand up and do the part that has your name on it.

That is not a lack of faith. It may be the first evidence of faith.

Prayer can quiet the noise long enough for you to hear the truth. It can remind you that you are not carrying the whole world. It can bring fear, grief, anger, and confusion into the light.

But prayer is not a hiding place from the phone call, apology, application, boundary, appointment, or hour of work you already know is necessary.

You can ask God for direction while refusing to take the first visible step. You can pray for peace while feeding the same chaos. You can ask for an open door and then stay seated when it opens.

This morning, pray. Then move.

## Faith does not require you to control the outcome

Some people avoid action because they cannot guarantee the result.

If I make the call, what if they say no? If I apologize, what if they do not forgive me? If I apply, what if I am rejected? If I tell the truth, what if it costs me something?

Faith does not promise control over those answers.

It asks whether you will be faithful with the choice in front of you.

Your responsibility may be to make the call. The answer belongs to someone else. Your responsibility may be to tell the truth. The consequence is not fully yours to manage. Your responsibility may be to begin the work. Finishing it may require help, time, and another plan.

Do not confuse uncertainty with a closed door.

If guilt is keeping you frozen, use the difference between [regret and actual repair](https://realryannichols.com/posts/guilt-is-not-the-same-as-repair). Feeling bad is not the finish line. One honest correction is stronger than another hour of private punishment.

## Prayer should make the next step clearer, not grander

We often want a complete map.

We ask for the five-year plan when today's instruction is already sitting on the table. We want a dramatic sign when the next move is ordinary: answer the email, pay what you can, stop sending the message, go to sleep, ask for help, show up on time.

The next faithful step may not look spiritual.

It may look like a calendar reminder.

It may look like deleting a number, washing the dishes, setting a boundary, filling out a form, or admitting that you do not know what to do next.

That is still part of the work.

Faith is not measured by how dramatic the moment feels. Sometimes it is measured by whether you do the quiet necessary thing after the prayer ends.

## The 10-minute pray-and-move reset

Use this when your thoughts are loud and the day already feels heavy.

### Minutes 1 through 3: Put down the performance

Tell God the truth in plain language.

No speech. No attempt to sound strong. Name what you fear, what you want, what you regret, and what you do not understand.

If you are angry, say it. If you are exhausted, say it. If you have been avoiding something, say that too.

Honest prayer does not need decoration.

### Minutes 4 through 5: Ask one useful question

Ask:

**What is the next honest step that still belongs to me?**

Not the whole solution. Not somebody else's responsibility. Not the outcome you wish you could force.

Your next step.

### Minutes 6 through 8: Write the action small

Finish this sentence:

**Before ________, I will ________.**

Make it visible and measurable.

- Before 9:00, I will send the update.
- Before lunch, I will make the appointment.
- Before I open social media, I will work for twenty minutes.
- Before tonight, I will ask one person for help.
- Before the conversation, I will write the facts without insults.

If you keep renegotiating the same responsibility, return to [the first necessary thing](https://realryannichols.com/posts/stop-renegotiating-the-first-necessary-thing). The goal is not to feel fearless. The goal is to stop making fear the decision-maker.

### Minutes 9 through 10: Begin before the feeling changes

Open the document. Dial the number. Put on the shoes. Send the first sentence. Move the money. Set the boundary.

Do not wait for a surge of motivation. Start while your hands still feel uncertain.

## Waiting can also be an honest step

Action does not always mean pushing.

Sometimes the faithful move is to wait without interfering. You have made the request. You have provided the information. You have apologized. You have done the safe preparation. There is nothing useful to force today.

Waiting with responsibility is different from avoidance.

Responsible waiting has a check-in time, a protected obligation, and a decision about what you will do while the answer is outside your control. Avoidance keeps everything vague and calls the anxiety a plan.

If you are depleted, [responsible rest may be part of the assignment](https://realryannichols.com/posts/rest-is-part-of-the-assignment). Rest can be faithful when it protects your responsibilities and includes a return.

## Your move this Sunday

Pray for what you cannot control.

Ask for wisdom about what you can.

Then put one honest step on the clock.

You do not need to fix your entire life before breakfast. You do not need to know how every part will end. You need enough courage for the piece of obedience, responsibility, or repair that belongs to this day.

What will exist after your prayer that did not exist before it?
