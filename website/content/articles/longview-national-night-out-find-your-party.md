Longview’s National Night Out is Tuesday, October 6.

The city describes it as one night focused on making Longview safer and more connected. The basic idea is simple: neighbors gather, meet each other, build relationships with local police, and talk about crime prevention before a crisis forces the introduction.

The event is citywide, but it is not one single festival with one universal start time.

That detail matters.

If you want to participate, find your neighborhood gathering before you leave home.

## What the city has confirmed

The [official City of Longview event page](https://www.longviewtexas.gov/Calendar.aspx?EID=10840&calType=0&day=3&month=10&year=2026) lists National Night Out for Tuesday, October 6, 2026. The city says residents are encouraged to host block parties, invite neighbors, strengthen police-community partnerships, and share crime-prevention information.

The same page links to an [official 2026 party map](https://cloud.longviewtexas.gov/maps/NationalNightOut2026/) where residents can look for a gathering near them.

Longview also posted a public notice indicating that City Council members may attend National Night Out events. That notice confirms the date, but it does not turn every gathering into a formal council meeting or guarantee that an elected official will be at every party.

## What remains different from neighborhood to neighborhood

The city calendar labels the event as all day because the gatherings are distributed across Longview. It does not publish one citywide start and finish time on the main event page.

Individual parties may differ in:

- location and boundaries;
- start and end time;
- food, seating, or activities;
- street access and parking;
- whether registration or a neighborhood invitation is expected; and
- whether police, fire, city staff, or elected officials are scheduled to stop by.

Do not assume the details for one party apply to the next neighborhood.

The map is the starting point. The host or listing attached to your location is the next check.

## Who this is useful for

National Night Out is not only for people who run a neighborhood association.

It can be useful if you just moved to Longview, if an older neighbor lives alone, if your block has dealt with vehicle break-ins, if families want a safer way for children to know the adults nearby, or if you have questions about when to call police and when to use a non-emergency channel.

It can also be useful when nothing dramatic is wrong.

Community is easier to build before the emergency.

Knowing which house has a nurse, who owns the loose dog, who works nights, who has a ladder, and who will notice an unfamiliar vehicle is ordinary information. It becomes valuable when weather, fire, crime, or a missing person turns ordinary into urgent.

That is the public value of the night. Not a speech. Not a photo opportunity. A few more people knowing who is actually beside them.

## A five-minute plan before Tuesday

### 1. Open the official map

Use the [City of Longview’s National Night Out map](https://cloud.longviewtexas.gov/maps/NationalNightOut2026/). Look for the closest registered gathering.

### 2. Verify the local details

Confirm the time, exact location, and whether the host has posted any instructions. If the map does not answer the question, contact the listed organizer or the city through an official channel.

### 3. Decide what you can contribute

Bring a chair if seating is uncertain. Carry water. Offer to help an older neighbor get there. Write down one practical neighborhood question instead of waiting until the conversation is over.

### 4. Protect privacy

Meeting neighbors does not require sharing private schedules, alarm details, access codes, or personal information. Useful connection and responsible privacy can exist together.

### 5. Leave with one real contact

One trusted neighbor’s name and number is more useful than a stack of flyers you will not read.

Ask before adding anyone to a group message. Keep the contact for a clear neighborhood purpose.

## If there is no nearby party

The official map may change as registrations are added or corrected. A missing marker does not prove that nothing is happening in your area.

Check the map again, ask your neighborhood association if one exists, or use the city’s official contact information to ask how to participate. Do not create your own public event listing using somebody else’s address or name without permission.
