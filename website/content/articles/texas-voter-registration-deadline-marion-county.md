Today is the line.

The Texas Secretary of State says Monday, October 5, 2026 is the last day to register to vote for the November 3 general election.

That does not mean you should post your status online. It does not mean sending a photo of a registration card to prove anything to anybody. Voter records contain personal details that belong in the official process, not in a comment thread.

It means checking the right place, using an authorized method if something needs to change, and keeping a non-sensitive receipt.

This information was verified against official Texas and Marion County sources at 3:41 a.m. Central on October 5, 2026.

## First, check whether the record is already correct

The official [VoteTexas registration page](https://www.votetexas.gov/register-to-vote/) links to the state’s “Am I Registered?” portal. The portal lets a voter check the statewide registration database.

Look at the county, name, and address information shown through the official system. Do not rely on a campaign list, a social-media message, or an old card in a drawer when the state portal is available.

If the record is correct, save a simple confirmation for yourself. Do not publish the screen. Do not send voter identifiers to strangers.

If the record is missing or the address is wrong, keep moving.

## A new Texas application filled out online is not automatically submitted

This is where people can lose the day.

The Texas registration FAQ says a person can fill out an application online, but a new application must still be printed and sent to the county voter registrar. The [official registration FAQ](https://www.votetexas.gov/faq/registration.html) says the deadline can be the date the application is received by the registrar or the postmark date.

The state’s [registration page](https://www.votetexas.gov/register-to-vote/) adds a warning about mail processing. It recommends taking a mailed application to a United States Postal Service retail counter and requesting a free manual local postmark so the date matches the day it was presented.

That detail matters on deadline day.

An online address change is different. VoteTexas says Texans who are already registered and recently moved or changed their name may be able to update registration information through the official online process. Use the state’s [update-voter-registration page](https://www.votetexas.gov/register-to-vote/update-voter-registration.html) to see the authorized options that apply to your situation.

## Marion County contacts and local verification

Texas voter registration is county-based. The state directs voters with record-specific questions to the county voter registrar.

Marion County’s official [elections page](https://www.co.marion.tx.us/page/marion.Elections) lists the county elections office at 119 West Lafayette in Jefferson and publishes the office phone as 903-665-3281. That page also links back to official Texas voter information.

Office information can change. Check the county page before driving, and call the number shown on the official page if you need to confirm where a registration application must be delivered.

The point is not to guess which county employee handles which form. The point is to enter through the official county door and get a direct answer.

## The deadline-day checklist

Use this in order:

1. Open the official Texas voter-registration page and check your record.
2. Confirm the county and address without sharing the result publicly.
3. If a new application is needed, complete it through an authorized state or county method.
4. If mailing today, go to a USPS retail counter and request the dated manual postmark described by the state.
5. If delivering in person, confirm the correct Marion County office and hours first.
6. Keep a receipt, dated postmark, or non-sensitive confirmation.
7. Recheck the official state portal after the county has had time to process the record.

Do not hand a registration form to a random person because they say they will take care of it. Do not send a photograph of the completed form through an unsecured message. Do not treat an online form preview as proof that a new application reached the registrar.

## What happens after today

The Texas Secretary of State’s [2026 election dates](https://www.sos.state.tx.us/elections/candidates/guide/2026/dates2026.shtml) list October 19 as the start of early voting and October 30 as the last day of early voting for the November 3 election.

If a registered Texas voter moved to a new county and missed the new-county registration deadline, the state says that person may qualify for a limited ballot during early voting. The rules are narrower than ordinary voting. The [state’s registration FAQ](https://www.votetexas.gov/faq/registration.html) explains the basic eligibility and directs voters to the limited-ballot process.

The safest move is still to handle the registration question today.
