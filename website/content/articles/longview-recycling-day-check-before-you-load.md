Longview has a four-hour window this morning for residents trying to clear recyclable material out of a garage, cabinet, or back seat.

The [City of Longview calendar](https://longviewtexas.gov/Calendar.aspx?EID=10964&calType=0&day=3&month=10&year=2026) lists Keep Longview Beautiful Recycling Day for Saturday, October 3, from 8 a.m. to noon at Longview Mall, 3500 McCann Road.

That is the useful part.

Here is the part that needs care: the city notice names the event and partners, but it does not publish a complete accepted-material list on that page.

Do not load the truck based on a guess.

## What the city has confirmed

As verified at 3:50 a.m. Central on October 3, the official event notice gives these facts:

- **What:** Community Recycling Day
- **When:** Saturday, October 3, 2026, from 8 a.m. to noon
- **Where:** Longview Mall, 3500 McCann Road, Longview
- **Partners named:** Keep Longview Beautiful, Interstate All Battery, LCM, and various material-recycling partners

The city calendar also lists the general City Hall phone number as **903-237-1000**.

The event page does not state whether it will accept televisions, monitors, loose lithium-ion batteries, paint, tires, appliances, paper for shredding, fluorescent bulbs, chemicals, or every kind of household battery.

That uncertainty is not a reason to skip a useful event. It is a reason to verify the load before leaving home.

## Use a three-question check before you drive

Ask these questions for each material:

1. **Is this item named by the organizer today?**
2. **Does it need special handling because it contains a battery, chemical, refrigerant, or personal data?**
3. **Can I transport it safely without leaks, loose terminals, broken glass, or exposed parts?**

If the answer is unclear, use the [official event listing](https://longviewtexas.gov/Calendar.aspx?EID=10964&calType=0&day=3&month=10&year=2026) and the city contact number before you make the trip.

This is especially important for batteries and electronics.

## Do not put loose lithium batteries in ordinary recycling

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency says used lithium-ion batteries should not go in household trash or municipal recycling bins. The agency recommends specialized battery collection sites, electronics recyclers, participating retailers, or household hazardous-waste programs.

For removable household batteries, the EPA's [used-battery guidance](https://www.epa.gov/recycle/used-household-batteries) recommends placing each battery in a separate plastic bag or covering the terminals with nonconductive tape before transport.

The reason is practical. Exposed terminals can contact metal and create a fire risk during storage, hauling, or sorting.

For a phone, laptop, tool, e-bike component, or another device with a battery built in, do not force the battery out unless the product is designed for safe removal. The EPA says the complete device can go to a certified electronics recycler or an appropriate take-back program.

The presence of Interstate All Battery among today's named partners suggests batteries are part of the event's work. It does not prove that every battery type or every battery-powered device will be accepted. The event organizer remains the source of truth for today's load.

## Protect your information before recycling electronics

If the event confirms that it accepts phones, computers, tablets, or storage devices, deal with your data before the handoff.

- Back up anything you need.
- Sign out of accounts.
- Remove SIM and memory cards when appropriate.
- Use the manufacturer's reset process.
- Remove labels that expose personal information.
- Keep a note of the device and where you surrendered it.

A reset does not replace careful account security. Change important passwords if a device cannot be cleared or if you are not sure what remains on it.

## Pack the vehicle for an orderly handoff

Community collection lines work better when people sort before arriving.

- Group like materials together.
- Keep batteries separate from metal objects.
- Put small loose items in sturdy containers.
- Do not combine unknown liquids.
- Keep sharp or broken items contained.
- Leave room to unload without climbing over unstable stacks.

If volunteers reject an item, do not leave it beside a bin or on the property. Take it home and ask the city or Gregg County where it can be handled safely.

The [Longview Fourth Street corridor article](/posts/longview-fourth-street-corridor-survey) makes a broader point about public participation: local systems work better when residents use the official channel and bring specific information. Recycling events are the same. A clean, confirmed load is more useful than a truck full of hopeful guesses.

## What remains uncertain

The public notice is clear about the time, place, and partner organizations. It is not clear about the full material list, quantities, residency limits, fees, or whether the line may close early if capacity is reached.

Those details can change how useful the event is for a particular household.

Check first. Arrive early enough to ask questions. Follow the instructions at the site.

And if you learn that a common item is not accepted, send the official answer through the site's tip line so the next update can help another East Texas household.
