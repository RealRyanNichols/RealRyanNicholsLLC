Emergency preparation usually gets described as a pile of supplies.

Water. Flashlights. Batteries. Medicine. A weather radio.

Longview residents have another practical tool available before the next warning arrives: the city’s Smart911 emergency alert system and its optional Safety Profile.

The [City of Longview’s official emergency-alert page](https://www.longviewtexas.gov/2864/Emergency-Alerts) says residents can choose voice calls, text messages, email alerts, or all three. The city says the regional notification system operates through a partnership with the East Texas Council of Governments.

That alert setting is the first job.

The Safety Profile is the second.

## What the profile can hold

The city says residents may add information that could help first responders during an emergency call, including:

- allergies, medications, or mobility needs
- the number of pets in the home
- a house description or gate code that could speed access

The city describes the information as private and secure and says it is shared with first responders during an emergency call.

That does not mean every household needs to enter every available field. It means each resident can decide whether a detail would help responders understand a real access, health, mobility, or household issue when time is tight.

Start with this question: What would be hard to explain clearly during the first minute of an emergency?

That is the information worth considering.

## Who may benefit most

The system can be useful for any resident who wants local emergency notifications. The profile may be especially useful for households where one missing detail could slow a response.

That can include:

- a person who uses a wheelchair or other mobility equipment
- someone with a serious allergy
- a household behind a gate
- a home that is difficult to identify from the street
- a resident who relies on a landline instead of a smartphone
- a home with pets responders should expect to encounter

The city’s page specifically lists voice calls to mobile phones or landlines, SMS text messages, and email. Residents can choose more than one channel.

Redundancy matters. A phone may be silenced. A text may arrive when the network is congested. An email may be easier to reread when instructions are detailed. Choosing multiple channels gives one warning more than one route to reach you.

## What remains uncertain

An emergency profile cannot guarantee a particular response, a particular arrival time, or uninterrupted service during a disaster.

It also does not replace calling 911 in an emergency. The city lists 903-237-1170 for non-emergency Public Safety Communications matters and says to call 911 to report an emergency.

Technology changes. Phone numbers change. Medications change. People move. A profile that was accurate two years ago may be less useful today.

Treat registration as the beginning, not the finish line.

Review your contact methods after a move or phone-number change. Review household details when access, medical, mobility, or pet information changes. Remove information that no longer belongs there.

## How to sign up or get help

The city provides an [online Smart911 registration link](https://www.smart911.com/smart911/ref/reg.action?pa=longviewtx) from its official page, plus links to the Apple and Android apps.

Residents who do not have internet access or want help registering can visit the Longview Public Library. The city says library staff provide one-on-one registration assistance.

Use this five-step setup:

1. Open the city’s official emergency-alert page and follow its registration link.
2. Choose the notification methods you will actually notice.
3. Add only the Safety Profile details that would help during an emergency.
4. Ask another adult in the household to review the contact and access information.
5. Put a reminder on the calendar to review the profile in six months.

Do not wait for thunder to start searching for the sign-up page.

## Make one household decision today

Preparation works better when it is specific.

Not “we should sign up sometime.”

Choose the person who will create the account. Choose which phones and emails should receive alerts. Decide whether the household has an access or mobility detail worth adding. Then complete it while the day is quiet.
