*By the Real Ryan Nichols Editorial Team*

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has run one of the most valuable companies on earth through the entire AI boom without ever posting on X. That changed on July 24, 2026 — and he didn't use his debut post for a product launch or an earnings tease. He used it to back an open letter on why open-weight AI models matter.

The post is real and public: [x.com/JensenHuang/status/2080643682408321103](https://x.com/JensenHuang/status/2080643682408321103). The confirmed text of his first tweet ever:

> "For my first post, I'm sharing a letter @NVIDIA signed on why open models matter. AI will transform every industry, power every company, and be built by every country. Open models strengthen safety and cybersecurity, accelerate innovation and diffusion, and enable sovereignty. The world needs both frontier closed models and frontier open models."

That's confirmed by [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/tech/26/07/60671067/jensen-huang-posts-on-x-for-the-first-time-ever-and-uses-it-to-defend-open-source-ai), [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/07/24/jensen-huang-open-source-letter-nvidia-kimi/), [Cryptopolitan](https://www.cryptopolitan.com/nvidia-ceo-huang-backs-open-model/) and [Geo.tv](https://www.geo.tv/latest/674626-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-makes-x-debut-with-urgent-open-source-ai-plea).

## Who signed, and who didn't

Reporting agrees the letter carries roughly 25 signatories, with Nvidia, Microsoft, Meta, Palantir and Hugging Face confirmed across multiple outlets. The exact full list varies slightly by which outlet is reporting it, so I'm not going to hand you a definitive company-by-company roster beyond the names every source agrees on — check Nvidia's own newsroom post if you want the complete letter.

The detail getting the most attention isn't who signed. It's who didn't: OpenAI and Anthropic are both reported as absent from the list. Those are two of the highest-profile labs building frontier closed models, so their absence reads, fairly or not, as a quiet statement in itself.

## Why the timing isn't a coincidence

This letter didn't land in a vacuum. It's arriving in the middle of a live U.S.-China competitive dynamic around AI models, with open-weight releases like DeepSeek and Kimi putting real pressure on the assumption that the most capable models have to stay closed. Huang's framing leans into that directly — sovereignty, safety, and diffusion of capability across countries, not just companies.

Coming from the CEO whose chips power basically every side of this race, that's not a neutral technical opinion. Nvidia sells the hardware regardless of whether the winning approach is open or closed — but a world with more open models generally means more players buying compute to run and fine-tune them, rather than a handful of closed labs consolidating demand. It's worth naming that incentive plainly rather than pretending Huang's post is disinterested philosophy.

## What's actually new here versus recycled debate

Open-versus-closed AI is not a new argument. What's new is the messenger and the moment: the most recognizable hardware executive in AI, choosing his literal first public word on X to plant a flag on one side of it, at a moment when the open-model camp has real technical momentum instead of just an ideological one.

That combination — timing, messenger, and a pointed omission of two major labs — is why this is spreading further than a typical corporate letter would.
