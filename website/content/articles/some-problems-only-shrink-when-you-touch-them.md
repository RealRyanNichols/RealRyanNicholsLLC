Some problems get smaller with time.

Most of the problems keeping you awake do not.

The unpaid bill does not become easier because you stopped opening the envelope. The difficult conversation does not become safer because both people learned to avoid it. The application does not submit itself. The mistake does not turn into repair while you rehearse explanations in your head.

Avoidance can buy an hour of relief. It often charges interest.

That is the hard truth this morning: some problems only shrink when you touch them.

## You do not have to solve it to make contact

People delay hard things because they imagine the first action must also be the final answer.

If you look at the debt, you must somehow pay all of it. If you call the doctor, you must already know what is wrong. If you open the project, you must finish it. If you admit the mistake, you must have the perfect repair plan.

That standard turns contact into a threat.

Contact is smaller than completion.

It may mean reading the whole notice instead of guessing what it says. It may mean asking for the balance, gathering the dates, opening the file, writing the first honest sentence, or scheduling the meeting. The problem may still be there when you finish. The difference is that you will be working with facts instead of fear.

Facts can be organized. Fear keeps changing shape.

## Avoidance creates work you cannot see

Avoidance feels like doing nothing. It is not.

You spend energy remembering what not to think about. You check your phone but avoid one message. You move the same task to tomorrow. You build backup explanations. You stay busy enough to say you had no time.

That hidden work is exhausting because it produces no evidence of progress.

This is why [An Excuse Can Be True and Still Keep You Stuck](/posts/an-excuse-can-be-true-and-still-keep-you-stuck) matters. You may have a real reason the problem is difficult. The reason deserves respect. It does not deserve permanent control.

The first honest action does not deny the difficulty. It stops feeding it.

## Touch the part you can verify

When a problem feels too large, separate what is known from what is imagined.

Known facts have dates, amounts, names, requirements, and consequences you can confirm. Imagined facts usually begin with words like probably, always, everybody, ruined, or impossible.

Write both lists.

Then choose an action that produces one new verified fact.

Call and ask what the actual deadline is. Check the account balance. Read the rule. Ask the person what they meant. Put every missing document in one folder. Request help before the emergency makes the decision for you.

[Ask for Help Before the Problem Chooses for You](/posts/ask-for-help-before-the-problem-chooses-for-you) is not permission to hand your responsibility to somebody else. It is a reminder that responsible people use available help before the cost of silence gets higher.

## Use the 20-minute contact plan

Do not spend the next hour designing a complete recovery system. Set a timer for twenty minutes.

1. **Name one problem.** Write one plain sentence without defending yourself or predicting disaster.
2. **Write the next consequence.** What happens if you make no contact for seven more days?
3. **Separate fact from fear.** List three things you know and three things you are assuming.
4. **Choose one contact action.** Open, call, ask, schedule, pay, send, or gather.
5. **Create evidence.** Save the confirmation, write the date, photograph the receipt, or note exactly what was said.
6. **Set the next checkpoint.** Decide when you will return and what must be ready then.

The plan is not complete until an action outside your head has happened.

Research can be useful, but it can also become a cleaner form of hiding. A new notebook is not contact. A motivational video is not contact. Telling five people what you plan to do is not the same as doing the first part.

If you keep renegotiating the start, read [Give the Hard Task a Start Time](/posts/give-the-hard-task-a-start-time) and place the contact action on the clock.

## One honest touch changes your position

The problem may still be serious after twenty minutes.

You may learn that the amount is larger, the deadline is closer, or the conversation will be harder than you hoped. That information can sting. It is still better than letting imagination run the case without evidence.

Once you make contact, you are no longer only somebody carrying a problem. You are somebody producing the next fact, the next record, and the next move.

That is forward motion.

Not a speech. Not a promise that everything will work out. One honest point of contact with the thing you have been avoiding.
