A useful Friday afternoon plan does not have to cost anything.

The City of Longview lists a free Teen Esports Game Day for Friday, October 2, from 4:15 to 5:45 p.m. at Broughton Recreation Center. The session is for young people ages 13 to 17, and the city’s event page says a recreation center membership is not required.

That is a small public offering with practical value. It gives teens a supervised place to gather around a shared interest after school, and it gives families a no-cost option that does not require a long drive out of Longview.

The details below were verified against the [City of Longview event listing](https://www.longviewtexas.gov/Calendar.aspx?EID=10889) on October 2, 2026.

## The confirmed details

The city lists:

- **What:** Teen Esports Game Day
- **When:** Friday, October 2, 4:15 to 5:45 p.m.
- **Where:** Broughton Recreation Center Esports Game Room
- **Address:** 801 South Martin Luther King Jr. Boulevard, Longview, Texas 75602
- **Ages:** 13 to 17
- **Cost:** Free
- **Membership:** No recreation center membership required
- **Contact:** 903-237-1345 or JClay@longviewtexas.gov

The listing describes the program as a Friday esports opportunity, so families interested in later dates should check the city calendar rather than assume every future Friday follows the same schedule.

## What the listing does not answer

The official page does not identify the available games, the number of gaming stations, whether advance sign-up is needed, or how the center handles capacity if more teens arrive than the room can serve.

Those details can change the plan for a family. A parent may also want to ask about check-in, pickup, behavior expectations, accessibility, and whether a teen should bring anything.

The safest move is simple: call 903-237-1345 before leaving if any of those questions matter to you. The public listing provides the basic invitation, not every operating detail.

## Why a program like this has public value

Esports are still games, and families can make their own decisions about screen time. But the word “gaming” can hide what a well-run community session may actually provide.

Teenagers get a place to practice communication, learn rules, manage winning and losing, and meet people outside their immediate circle. A recreation center gets a chance to reach young people who may not walk through the door for basketball, crafts, or a traditional club.

None of that guarantees a perfect program. It does explain why a city recreation department would treat an esports room as community space instead of just a row of screens.

## A quick checklist before you go

If a teen in your household wants to attend, use this five-minute check:

1. Confirm the teen is between 13 and 17.
2. Confirm transportation to and from Broughton Recreation Center.
3. Call ahead if game selection, accessibility, capacity, or pickup rules could affect the decision.
4. Make sure the teen knows the agreed pickup time.
5. Check the [official city calendar](https://www.longviewtexas.gov/Calendar.aspx?EID=10889) once more for any late change.

No membership and no listed fee remove two common barriers. Transportation and timely information can still be barriers, especially for families balancing work schedules.
