A spreadsheet class will not solve every employment problem in East Texas.

It can remove one practical barrier.

The City of Longview’s official calendar lists a free Basic Excel II class for Wednesday, September 23, from 9:00 a.m. to 12:30 p.m. in the Longview Public Library PC Computer Lab at 222 West Cotton Street.

The listing says signup is required and Basic Excel I is a prerequisite.

By the time this article publishes at 10:00 a.m., the scheduled class will already be underway. Do not leave home assuming a seat is available. Call the library at 903-237-1351 to verify the current class or ask when the next session will be offered.

## What the official listing says

The [City of Longview calendar](https://www.longviewtexas.gov/Calendar.aspx?EID=10929&calType=0&day=23&month=9&year=2026) describes a hands-on second-level Excel class.

The posted topics include:

- AutoFill and column work
- Table formatting and Format Painter
- Formulas and basic functions
- AutoSum and conditional formatting
- Find and Replace
- Flash Fill and subtotals
- Filters and data validation
- Headers, footers, and page breaks

Those are not advanced data-science skills. They are everyday office skills.

A small business may use them to track inventory, build a quote, clean a contact list, or prepare a weekly report. A job applicant may encounter them in an administrative, customer-service, warehouse, nonprofit, or public-sector role. A household may use the same tools for a budget or project list.

## Who may benefit

The class is most relevant to people who have completed the library’s Basic Excel I course or already have equivalent beginner knowledge and can confirm the prerequisite with staff.

That may include:

- A job seeker who sees “Excel preferred” in local listings
- A worker moving from the field into scheduling or office support
- A small-business owner who has been keeping records by hand
- A volunteer responsible for a club or nonprofit roster
- A resident who wants structured practice without paying for a private course

The public value is not only that the class is free. It is that the training is local, in a computer lab, and tied to tasks people actually meet.

## What remains uncertain

An official calendar listing is not a guarantee that a late-arriving student can enter after the class begins.

The page does not establish that seats remain open at publication time. It does not say whether walk-ins are accepted. It also does not list the next Basic Excel I or Basic Excel II date.

Those details need direct confirmation from the library.

The calendar provides a contact number, 903-237-1351, and lists BCHunter@longviewtexas.gov as the event contact email.

## If you missed this morning’s start

Missing one class time does not mean the opportunity is gone.

Use a short, specific call:

1. Ask whether today’s Basic Excel II class is still accepting registered participants.
2. If not, ask for the next Basic Excel I and Basic Excel II dates.
3. Confirm how registration works.
4. Ask what skills are expected before Excel II.
5. Put the date and prerequisite on your calendar before the call ends.

If you already know the basics, ask whether the library keeps a cancellation list or offers other computer classes. Do not assume. Verify.

## Make a free class produce a real result

Training is more useful when it ends with an artifact.

After a class, build one spreadsheet you can keep using. It could be a simple expense tracker, a follow-up list, a volunteer schedule, or a job-application log.

Use at least three tools from the lesson. For example:

- Create a formatted table.
- Add a basic formula or AutoSum.
- Apply a filter.
- Use data validation to limit an entry.
- Add a header and prepare the sheet for printing.

Then save a clean copy as a template.

That turns a morning class into a repeatable system.

## Why small public resources matter

Public libraries are often treated as places to borrow books and use Wi-Fi.

They are also local access points for equipment, instruction, and patient help.

A resident who cannot justify a paid course may be able to attend a free lab. A person who feels behind can practice in a room where learning is the entire reason for being there. A small-business owner can pick up one skill that reduces a recurring mess.

That is practical public value.

It is not a promise of a new job or a better business. It is a chance to leave with more control than you had before.
