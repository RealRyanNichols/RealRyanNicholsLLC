This week X wasn't fighting about politics, sports contracts, or who got suspended for what. It was passing around a raccoon.

His name is Jimothy. He lives in the Ballard neighborhood of Seattle, and he does not look like other raccoons. His body is compact, almost stubby, his neck barely turns, and he moves with a kind of low, determined waddle that looks nothing like the animal you're picturing when you hear "raccoon." Wildlife watchers believe — unofficially, since nobody's been able to examine him up close — that he has something called Short Spine Syndrome, a condition that shortens the spine and limits flexibility in the neck and body.

Ballard residents have been watching him for a while: crossing backyard decks, drinking out of a dog's water bowl, sitting in a tree like he owns the block. Someone posted a clip. Then everyone posted a clip. Within days, Jimothy had racked up millions of views across X, TikTok, and Instagram, and local Seattle outlets were covering a raccoon like it was a city council vote. [MyBallard has the full rundown of how a neighborhood curiosity turned into an internet sensation.](https://www.myballard.com/2026/07/16/jimothy-the-short-spined-raccoon-from-ballard-becomes-an-internet-sensation/)

## Why a raccoon beat the algorithm

I write about heavy things on this site more often than not. So let me say the quiet part: I think it's worth stopping to notice when the internet agrees on something for once, and that something is "be nice to the weird little guy."

Nobody's fighting about Jimothy. Nobody's demanding he apologize for how he looks or questioning his motives. People are just... rooting for him. Wildlife officials confirmed he seems capable of finding food, climbing, and getting around fine on his own — he's not suffering, he's just built differently — and that turned out to be exactly the permission slip the internet needed to fall in love with him without guilt.

That's rarer than it should be. Most of what goes viral runs on outrage, because outrage is the cheapest fuel there is. Jimothy went viral on the opposite fuel: pure, uncomplicated affection for a creature that didn't ask to be famous and doesn't know it is.

## What I think this actually says

I don't think this is really a story about a raccoon. I think it's a story about what people are starving for.

Give the same platform a wounded, funny-looking animal that's clearly getting by just fine, and people will build it a fan base, defend it in the replies, and check in on it like a neighbor. That instinct — to protect something small and different instead of mocking it — is in all of us. It just doesn't get much airtime most days.

If Jimothy is smart enough to keep finding his way to that dog bowl every night, he's smart enough to have accidentally taught a very online, very divided platform how to agree on something again, even if it's just for a week.
