Longview has a community benefit tonight that puts education and animal rescue in the same room.

The City of Longview's [official calendar listing](https://www.longviewtexas.gov/Calendar.aspx?EID=10849&calType=0&day=1&month=9&year=2026) says “Let's Glow Bingo” is scheduled for Thursday, September 3, at Maude Cobb Convention Center, 100 Grand Boulevard.

Doors are listed to open at 5:30 p.m. Bingo begins at 6 p.m.

The event benefits the Kilgore College Foundation and Longview Area Animal Rescue, according to the city listing.

If you are considering attending, the useful move is to verify space before you leave home.

## What the official listing confirms

The city describes the night as an adults-only event for people 21 and older. It lists bingo, games, raffles, silent and live auctions, food, and drinks.

The public calendar lists tables at $1,000 with seating for ten people. It also names sponsorship contact Merlyn Holmes at 903-988-7521 and mholmes@kilgore.edu.

That tells readers several important things:

- this is a benefit, not a regular public bingo session
- the posted purchase unit is a full table
- the event has an age restriction
- the organizer has a direct contact for current availability

The listing does not clearly state whether single seats, walk-up admission, or remaining tables are available on September 3. It also does not explain how proceeds are divided between the two beneficiaries.

Those are questions to ask, not gaps to fill with guesses.

## Who the benefit is designed to help

The [Kilgore College Foundation](https://www.kilgore.edu/additional-resources/alumni/kc-foundation/) supports the college through charitable giving and donor relationships. Its official page is the right place to verify current foundation contacts and ways to give outside the event.

The city listing also names Longview Area Animal Rescue as a beneficiary. The listing does not provide a detailed mission statement, current program list, or financial breakdown for that organization. Readers who want to understand the animal-rescue side should ask the organizer for the current beneficiary information rather than relying on a similarly named group found elsewhere online.

That distinction matters. Local organizations can have names that resemble groups in other cities or states. A useful community spotlight should direct support to the right place.

## Why a two-cause benefit can work

Education and animal rescue do not look like the same lane at first.

One serves students and the long-term workforce. The other responds to an immediate community need involving animals, owners, volunteers, and foster capacity.

Putting both causes in one event can introduce supporters to work they might not have encountered on their own. A college donor may learn about rescue needs. An animal advocate may discover how a local foundation supports access and opportunity.

The event still has to be transparent about what a ticket or table supports. Donors should be comfortable asking where proceeds go, whether part of a payment is tax-deductible, and who can provide a receipt. Those are normal questions for any benefit.

## What to do before 5:30

If you already have a table or invitation, confirm your group's arrival plan and the venue entrance. Maude Cobb is a large facility, and a five-minute coordination message can prevent ten people from arriving separately and wondering where to go.

If you do not have a reservation, contact the organizer first. Ask three direct questions:

1. Is there still space tonight?
2. Are individual seats or only full tables available?
3. What payment and check-in instructions should attendees use?

Do not send payment information to an account copied from an unverified social post. Use the city-listed contact to confirm the current process.

If the event is full, support does not have to stop there. Ask each beneficiary how to give directly, volunteer, or follow its next public event. The goal is not merely to get into one room. It is to help useful local work continue after the lights go off.

## What remains uncertain

As of verification on September 3, 2026, the official public listing does not confirm:

- remaining table availability
- whether walk-ins are accepted
- whether individual tickets are sold
- the final auction or game schedule
- the allocation of proceeds between beneficiaries

Event details can change on the day of a fundraiser. The organizer's current answer controls.
