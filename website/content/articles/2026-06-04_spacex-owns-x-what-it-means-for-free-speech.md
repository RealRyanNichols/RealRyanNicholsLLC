On May 6, 2026, SpaceX officially absorbed xAI — the company that owns X, the platform formerly known as Twitter.

That means that as of right now, the following are all controlled by the same person, under the same corporate umbrella:

- **X** — the social media platform used by hundreds of millions of people to share news, political speech, and personal accounts
- **Grok** — the AI built directly into X that can generate text, images, and soon video
- **Starlink** — the satellite internet infrastructure that provides connectivity to users in dozens of countries including active conflict zones
- **SpaceX** — one of the world's primary launch providers, with contracts from the U.S. government

Elon Musk called the combination "the most ambitious, vertically-integrated innovation engine on and off Earth, with AI, rockets, space-based internet, direct-to-mobile device communications and the world's foremost real-time information and free speech platform."

I want to sit with that last phrase for a second: **"the world's foremost real-time information and free speech platform."**

Because I have had my account on that platform locked or limited four separate times.

## What the merger actually means

Before the SpaceX deal, xAI already owned X. Musk completed that acquisition in March 2025, folding X Corp. into xAI in an all-stock deal that valued X at $33 billion.

The SpaceX acquisition — finalized May 6, 2026 — takes it further. Now xAI is a division of SpaceX. The combined company is the most valuable private company in the world, worth over $1.2 trillion according to Bloomberg.

That is not a social media company. That is an infrastructure company with a social media platform attached to it. The same entity that can potentially control whether a signal reaches a device in a remote area of the world also controls the content moderation rules of a major global speech platform.

That is a different kind of power than Twitter ever had.

## What this means for content moderation

Here is what I know from personal experience:

X has a content moderation system that can lock, limit, hide, or suppress accounts. I have been on the receiving end of that system while documenting public death threats, "traitors have no rights" language, and harassment campaigns directed at me. You can read the receipts [here](/posts/the-receipts-wall-every-threat-x-let-stay-up) and [here](/posts/every-day-they-tell-me-to-die).

The argument Musk has made for years is that X under his ownership is more committed to free speech than it was under the previous regime.

My receipts tell a more complicated story.

I am not saying the old Twitter was better. I am saying the claim of "free speech platform" needs to be tested against what actually happens when specific users post specific content — and the testing I have done shows a gap between the claim and the enforcement.

Under a vertically integrated empire that controls satellite internet, AI, and social media, that gap does not shrink automatically. It becomes more consequential.

## The part that should concern everyone

The merger is also notable because of what just happened on the regulatory front:

In February 2026, France raided X's offices and issued legal action against Musk for alleged violations of the Digital Services Act and related content moderation requirements. Australia also took action over Online Safety Act violations.

Those are government enforcement actions against a private speech platform. Meanwhile, that same platform is now owned by a company with U.S. government contracts for launch services and space infrastructure.

The relationship between government and this platform — in multiple jurisdictions, in multiple directions — is complicated. The consolidation makes it more complicated, not less.
