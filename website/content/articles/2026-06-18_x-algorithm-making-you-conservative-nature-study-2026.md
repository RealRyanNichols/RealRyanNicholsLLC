A peer-reviewed study published in *Nature* in February 2026 found something that should be front-page news.

Researchers from Bocconi University assigned active U.S.-based X users to two groups: one that used X's default "For You" algorithmic feed and one that switched to a simple chronological feed. They ran the experiment for seven weeks and measured political attitudes before, during, and after.

The results were stark.

Users on the algorithmic feed shifted toward more conservative political positions — specifically in their views on criminal investigations into Donald Trump and on the war in Ukraine. The algorithm was roughly 20% more likely to surface conservative posts and only 3.1% more likely to surface liberal ones. Traditional news appeared 58% fewer times in algorithmic feeds. Content from political activists appeared 27% more often.

And here is the part that makes this a genuinely significant scientific finding: **when researchers switched users back to a chronological feed, the political shift did not reverse.**

The effect persisted.

## This is not an opinion. This is a controlled experiment.

I want to be careful about how I frame this, because this is the kind of finding that gets weaponized by both sides.

This study was not conducted by a partisan think tank. It was published in *Nature* — one of the most rigorous peer-reviewed scientific journals on the planet. It used a randomized controlled experimental design, which is the gold standard for establishing cause and effect.

The study found that X's algorithm produces measurable political shifts in its users — and that those shifts outlast the algorithm itself.

If you are on the right, your instinct might be to celebrate this. If you are on the left, your instinct might be to call it proof of manipulation. I want to push back on both reactions.

This is not a win for any ideology. This is a documented case of a private platform reshaping how millions of people think about their government, their policies, and their world — without their knowledge, without their consent, and in ways they cannot easily undo.

## The "free speech" platform is not neutral

X markets itself — aggressively — as the free speech alternative to sanitized, over-moderated social media. Elon Musk built his entire brand around the acquisition on the premise that he was "restoring free speech" to the public square.

I've written before about the gap between a platform's stated values and its actual behavior. I've been on the receiving end of that gap.

The Nature study gives us a quantified version of that gap.

A truly neutral free speech platform would surface content across the political spectrum without systematic bias. Its algorithm would not reliably shift user opinion in one direction after seven weeks of exposure.

X's algorithm does not do that.

Conservative posts get a 20% algorithmic advantage. Traditional news sources get a 58% penalty. Political activists — disproportionately from the right, according to the data — get a 27% boost.

That is not a free speech platform. That is a platform with a political thumb on the scale.

## What this means if you use X every day

I use X. I post on X. I see what trends on X. And I have spent enough time watching the platform to understand that the "For You" feed is not showing me the world — it is showing me a curated version of the world that reinforces certain views and buries others.

The Nature study tells me that if you are on the default algorithmic feed right now, it has probably already shifted how you think about some political topics — and that shift will likely outlast your next attempt at a "news detox."

That is worth sitting with.

We talk constantly about media bias and the bias of individual journalists and the bias of cable news networks. We almost never talk seriously about the bias baked into the algorithm that decides what 300 million people see when they open their phones in the morning.

The algorithm is not neutral. It never was. And now we have peer-reviewed evidence of what it does.

## The specific findings on Trump and Ukraine

I want to call out two data points from the study because they are concrete and recent.

The algorithmic feed produced measurable shifts in users' views on the criminal investigations into Donald Trump — toward more favorable views of Trump and more skeptical views of the investigations.

It also produced shifts in users' views on the war in Ukraine — toward positions that were less supportive of Ukraine and more aligned with the positions of Trump's political movement.

These are not abstract "more conservative" findings. These are specific political questions with direct policy implications — and X's algorithm moved its users in a particular direction on both of them.

That happened to millions of people. Before this study, most of them had no idea it was happening to them.

The platform said it was neutral. The data says otherwise.
