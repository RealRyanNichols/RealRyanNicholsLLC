A free tree is not a flashy public program.

It is still one of the few giveaways that can keep working for a neighborhood long after the line is gone.

The City of Longview's official calendar says residents can take home one of 150 trees at the **TREEmendous Tree Giveaway** on Saturday, September 12, 2026. The event is scheduled for 9:00 to 11:00 a.m. on the Great Lawn at the Longview Arboretum & Nature Center, 706 West Cotton Street.

The city says five species will be available and distribution will be first come, first served. The calendar does not name those species or state what proof of residency will be requested.

That means the useful move is simple: read the [official event listing](https://www.longviewtexas.gov/Calendar.aspx?EID=10877&calType=0&day=7&month=9&year=2026), confirm any final details before Saturday, and arrive with a realistic plan for getting a tree home and into the ground.

## Who the giveaway is for

The city's language is specific: the trees are for **City of Longview residents**.

People who live outside city limits should not assume they qualify just because their mailing address says Longview. The official listing does not explain how residency will be checked, so call or use the city's event page before making a special trip if your address is close to the boundary.

The event is at the Great Lawn of the Longview Arboretum. The listed window is two hours, but “first come, first served” means the supply could run out before 11:00 a.m.

No reservation system, delivery service, or rain plan appears on the listing. Those details remain uncertain unless the city posts an update.

## Choose a place before you choose a tree

The best free tree is not automatically the biggest one or the first species you recognize.

Before Saturday, look at the space where you might plant it. Notice power lines, rooflines, driveways, sidewalks, fences, drainage, and the distance from the house. A small tree can become a large maintenance problem when it is planted in the wrong place.

Write down four facts:

- How many hours of direct sun the site receives
- Whether water stands there after heavy rain
- How far the trunk would be from pavement and structures
- Whether overhead or underground utilities affect the spot

Bring those facts with you. If volunteers can describe the mature size and needs of each species, you will be able to make a better choice than somebody selecting by appearance alone.

Do not dig until you have followed the applicable utility-location rules for your property. A free tree is not worth a damaged line or a dangerous guess.

## What to bring Saturday

The city listing does not publish a packing list, so this is practical preparation rather than an event requirement.

Consider bringing:

1. A vehicle with enough space for the tree without crushing branches
2. A tarp or old blanket to protect the vehicle
3. Soft ties to keep the tree stable without cutting into it
4. Water and closed-toe shoes if you expect to wait outdoors
5. Your address and a reasonable form of residency documentation, in case it is requested

Do not assume staff can load an oversized tree into an unsuitable vehicle. If transportation is a problem, coordinate with a neighbor before the event.

The official calendar is the controlling source. If details change, follow the city's update rather than a social-media screenshot.

## The public value is bigger than one yard

A well-placed tree can eventually offer shade, slow some stormwater, create habitat, and make a block more comfortable. Those benefits take time. They also depend on survival.

That is why the unglamorous steps matter: correct depth, water during establishment, mulch kept away from the trunk, and protection from equipment that can scar young bark.

If you receive a tree, ask for species-specific planting guidance. Do not copy a general internet instruction if the person distributing the tree provides better information for that species and local conditions.
