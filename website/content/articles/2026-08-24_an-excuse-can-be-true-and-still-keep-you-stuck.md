Some excuses are lies.

Some are facts.

The money really did run out. The person really did leave. The opportunity really was unfair. The call really did come at the worst possible time. You really are tired. You really were not given the tools, time, help, or clean beginning somebody else had.

That can all be true.

It can also keep you in the same place for another year if the explanation becomes the final answer.

An excuse does not have to be false to become dangerous. It only has to become more important than your next decision.

## The truth explains the wound. It does not choose the future

There is no strength in pretending the obstacle was imaginary.

If somebody broke trust, say that. If you made a bad decision, say that too. If the system failed, name the failure. If you were overwhelmed and stopped showing up, do not dress it in prettier language.

Honesty is not self-attack. It is accurate accounting.

But accurate accounting has two columns.

One column says what happened.

The other says what you will do now.

People get stuck when they keep adding detail to the first column and leave the second one blank.

[You Are Allowed to Learn the Lesson Late](https://realryannichols.com/posts/you-are-allowed-to-learn-the-lesson-late) is about accepting a lesson without spending the rest of your life paying shame interest on it. This is the next part of that work. The lesson becomes useful when it changes an action.

## A reason becomes an excuse when it cancels every option

There is a simple test.

Does the explanation help you see the problem clearly, or does it make every possible next step sound pointless?

“I cannot pay the whole balance today” may be true.

“So there is no reason to call, ask about terms, send twenty dollars, or make a plan” is surrender hiding behind the fact.

“I do not trust people after what happened” may be true.

“So I will never ask one safe person for one specific kind of help” gives the past permanent authority.

“I am exhausted” may be true.

“So the only choices are doing everything or doing nothing” is a false choice.

The hard truth is not that you should be able to do anything.

The hard truth is that there is usually something.

It may be smaller than your pride wants. It may not repair the whole problem. It may only stop the damage from spreading today.

That still counts.

## The 12 minute excuse-or-obstacle audit

Take one page. Do not audit your entire life. Choose the one problem you keep explaining but have not moved.

### Minutes 1 through 3: Write only the facts

Describe what is true without judgment, predictions, or courtroom language.

Write the amount missing. The deadline missed. The call not returned. The skill you do not have. The hour actually available.

Do not write “everything always falls apart.” That is a conclusion.

Write what a camera, calendar, receipt, or honest witness could confirm.

### Minutes 4 through 6: Circle what you cannot control

You may not control another person's answer, the old decision, the lost time, the market, the weather, or the final outcome.

Circle those things.

Then stop assigning them work on today's plan.

You are not agreeing that they are fair. You are refusing to build a plan that depends on changing what has already happened.

### Minutes 7 through 9: Underline what remains available

Look for one call, one dollar, one application, one hour, one apology, one boundary, one request, one page, or one person.

Small does not mean meaningless.

[Ask for the Help You Can Actually Name](https://realryannichols.com/posts/ask-for-the-help-you-can-actually-name) explains why a specific request gives another person something they can actually answer. “Help me fix my life” is fog. “Can you review this estimate before noon?” is a door.

### Minutes 10 through 12: Put the next action on a clock

Write when and where you will do the available thing.

Not “tomorrow.”

Write “at 8:40 a.m. at the kitchen table, I will call the number on the notice.”

The plan does not have to solve the whole situation. It has to create evidence that the explanation is no longer making every decision.

## Do not use compassion to keep yourself powerless

You deserve compassion for what hurt.

You also deserve a life that is not permanently organized around the injury.

Real compassion tells the whole truth. It says you were hit hard and you still have agency. It says your capacity may be limited without calling it zero. It lets you rest without calling disappearance recovery. It lets you admit fear without handing fear the schedule.

There is a version of kindness that never asks anything of you. It feels warm for a moment, but it leaves you exactly where it found you.

Better kindness brings water, sits beside you, and then helps you stand.

## Let the next action be smaller than the story

The story in your head may be ten years long.

The next action may take ten minutes.

That mismatch is normal.

You do not need an action as dramatic as the pain. You need an action honest enough to interrupt the pattern.

[Give the Hard Task a Start Time](https://realryannichols.com/posts/give-the-hard-task-a-start-time) makes the same point from a different direction. A task without a start time remains an argument. A hard truth without a next action remains a speech.

Finish the audit. Write the facts. Circle what you cannot control. Underline what remains. Put one action on the clock.

The explanation can stay true.

It just does not get to stay in charge.

What true explanation have you been treating like a permanent decision?
