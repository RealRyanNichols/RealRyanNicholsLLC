A report dropped this week from Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington — CREW — and the number at the top is real.

Ninety-seven pardoned January 6 defendants have been arrested, charged, or convicted of new crimes since receiving their pardons.

The crimes on the list are serious. Homicide. Burglary. Sexual assault on minors. Child pornography. Stalking. These are not misdemeanors. These are not parking tickets dressed up to score a political point.

I am not going to wave any of that away.

But I am going to tell you exactly what is being done with that number — because I have watched this playbook run before, and last time the number they weaponized was mine.

## Who CREW Is

Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington is a progressive watchdog organization. That is not a smear — it is a fact they disclose on their own website. They pursue government accountability, primarily against Republican officials. They file ethics complaints, litigate, and publish reports designed to move public and legal opinion.

That does not mean their data is wrong. It means you should understand the lens through which it was assembled and the purpose it was built to serve.

The purpose of this report is to argue that the pardons were a mistake. That is a legitimate argument to make. It also has an obvious political beneficiary.

## The Math They Don't Put in the Headline

More than 1,500 defendants received pardons related to January 6.

Ninety-seven is less than seven percent of that population.

CREW describes this as "one in 16 convicted rioters." What they do not say in the headline is that it is also ninety-three out of every hundred pardoned defendants who have not been arrested for anything since.

Seven percent is not nothing. Seven percent of people committing homicide and child sexual assault is not a number you shrug at. I am not shrugging at it.

But seven percent is also not "the J6 defendants." It is not "the people who went to the Capitol." It is not me. And the way this story is being framed — by every outlet that ran with the CREW report without adding that sentence — is designed to make you forget that distinction exists.

## I Will Not Defend the Ninety-Seven

Let me be direct about something some people reading this will test me on.

I am not going to defend anyone on that list. Not the person charged with homicide. Not the person charged with sexual assault on a child. Not one of them.

The pardon was for conduct related to January 6. It was not a character certification. It was not a guarantee that every person who received it would go on to live without further incident.

Some people on the pardon list should not have been pardoned. I believe that. I know specific situations where I have questions about what the person actually did on January 6 and whether the pardon fit the record. I have said as much in private. I am saying it publicly now, because I am not going to let this report force me into a defensive posture that requires me to cover for behavior I find indefensible.

Accountability cuts both ways. I have demanded it from the government for years. I am not stopping that demand when it points somewhere uncomfortable.

## What They Are Doing With the Number

Here is the part that is not acceptable.

The CREW report is being used — in editorial framing, in cable chyrons, in social media posts — to argue not just that 97 people were bad actors, but that the entire pardon decision was wrong, which means everyone who received one was undeserving, which means people like me belong back in a cell.

That is the logic. They will not say it that bluntly. But that is what the report is engineered to support.

I was charged for what I did on January 6. I have documented my own case, the evidence against me, and the evidence the government chose not to emphasize. I believe my prosecution was politically motivated. I believe the record supports that position. I documented it here, publicly, with sources you can read yourself.

The crimes of ninety-seven other people do not change what happened in my case. That is not a rationalization. That is how accountability is supposed to work — individual conduct judged on individual evidence.

## The Double Standard I Have to Name

CREW publishes reports on Republican officials.

I have not seen an equivalent CREW report on the prosecutors and agents who built J6 cases on contested evidence, who pushed for pretrial detention in conditions a federal judge later described as punitive, who had discovery management questions that were never fully answered in open court, and who made decisions — documented, public decisions — that would have ended a civilian career.

I have not seen the CREW accountability project aimed at the DC jail staff who operated the conditions I described in my own reporting. I have not seen the report on the agents who ran my investigation.

I would read those reports. I would share them. I would hold the people named in them to the same standard I hold the ninety-seven.

I am still waiting for CREW to write them.
